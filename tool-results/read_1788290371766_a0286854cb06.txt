     1→  2211→separated cost functionals.  Their proofs remain regime-specific:
     2→  2212→Myhill--Nerode theory, operator approximation, and information processing
     3→  2213→supply the analytical engines.

     6→  2216→\subsection{Master Scope: A Typed Variational Picture}
     7→  2217→\label{subsec:meta-scope}
     8→  2218→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
     9→  2219→
    10→  2220→The results of this manuscript, across commitment, retention, and grounding,
    11→  2221→instantiate one typed variational picture along four pillars.  Stating them
    12→  2222→together fixes what is unified and what is not; this subsection is a summary
    13→  2223→of scope, not a new derivation.
    14→  2224→
    15→  2225→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
    16→  2226→\item \textbf{Typed variational schema.}
    17→  2227→Every regime gap has the shape
    18→  2228→$\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=\inf_{\text{budget-}M\text{ feasible }B}
    19→  2229→\mathcal L(\delta,B)$,
    20→  2230→but the feasible class carries a regime-specific type: a finite right
    21→  2231→congruence on histories for commitment (Section~\ref{subsec:det-spec}), a
    22→  2232→stationary lumpable quotient for retention
    23→  2233→(Section~\ref{subsec:shared-schema-intro}), a deterministic Mealy machine for
    24→  2234→the symbolic grounding gap (Definition~\ref{def:symbolic-grounding-gap}), and
    25→  2235→a finite-rank bounded operator for the unrestricted linear grounding
    26→  2236→relaxation (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-grounding}).  The budget $M$ is a
    27→  2237→right-congruence index in the first three cases and an operator rank in the
    28→  2238→fourth; these are not asserted equivalent
    29→  2239→(Remark~\ref{rem:quotient-display-scope}).
    30→  2240→
    31→  2241→\item \textbf{Structural monotonicity and zero thresholds.}
    32→  2242→Each regime gap is non-increasing in $M$ and has an exact zero threshold on
    33→  2243→its own feasible class: the Myhill--Nerode index for commitment
    34→  2244→(Theorem~\ref{thm:commitment-spec}), the stationary support size of distinct
    35→  2245→predictive laws for full-KL retention (Theorem~\ref{thm:retention-zero}), and
    36→  2246→the operator rank of $H_\nu$ for the linear grounding relaxations
    37→  2247→(Proposition~\ref{prop:grounding-structured-zero}).  These thresholds are
    38→  2248→proved separately in each regime; no single argument produces all three.
    39→  2249→
    40→  2250→\item \textbf{Conditional response-operator converse.}
    41→  2251→Whenever a regime's approximants admit a response operator of bounded rank
    42→  2252→dominating the task cost (Definition~\ref{def:response}), the unified
    43→  2253→Schatten converse of Theorem~\ref{thm:unified} supplies a spectral-tail lower
    44→  2254→bound.  This bridge is conditional on the domination hypothesis and on the
    45→  2255→domination modulus, which may itself depend on the budget
    46→  2256→(Remark~\ref{rem:bridge-consequence}); it does not by itself make the three
    47→  2257→regimes' numerical gaps comparable (Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten-nogo}).
    48→  2258→
    49→  2259→\item \textbf{Regime-specific analytical engines.}
    50→  2260→The zero thresholds and quantitative rates are proved by different classical
    51→  2261→theories: Myhill--Nerode automata theory and minimax game values for
    52→  2262→commitment, information-bottleneck and Fisher-information asymptotics for
    53→  2263→retention, and Eckart--Young--Mirsky/Adamjan--Arov--Krein operator
    54→  2264→approximation for grounding.  The schema organizes these engines under a
    55→  2265→common typed vocabulary; it does not replace them.
    56→  2266→\end{enumerate}
    57→  2267→
    58→  2268→Sections~\ref{sec:commitment}--\ref{sec:grounding} develop each regime in
    59→  2269→this order, and Section~\ref{sec:joint} states precisely which joint and
    60→  2270→comparative statements the typed schema does and does not support across
    61→  2271→regimes.

    64→  2274→\subsection{Monotonicity and Profinite Convergence}
    65→  2275→\label{subsec:meta-monotone}
    66→  2276→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
    67→  2277→
    68→  2278→\begin{metatheorem}[Monotonicity and Profinite Convergence]
    69→  2279→\label{meta:monotone}
    70→  2280→Let $\mathbb T$ be a task theory.
    71→  2281→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    72→  2282→\item $\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)$ is non-increasing in $M$.
    73→  2283→\item If $\sim_\delta$ has finite index, then, $\mathcal E$ being reflexive by
    74→  2284→Definition~\ref{def:task-theory},
    75→  2285→\[
    76→  2286→\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=0
    77→  2287→\quad
    78→  2288→\forall M\ge\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta).
    79→  2289→\]
    80→  2290→\item If $\mathcal E$ is separated and $\{0,\infty\}$-valued, then:
    81→  2291→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    82→  2292→\item for worst-case aggregation,
    83→  2293→\[
    84→  2294→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)=0
    85→  2295→\text{ for some }M
    86→  2296→\iff
    87→  2297→\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)<\infty;
    88→  2298→\]
    89→  2299→\item for $\mu$-average aggregation,
    90→  2300→\[
    91→  2301→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0
    92→  2302→\text{ for some }M
    93→  2303→\iff
    94→  2304→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)<\infty,
    95→  2305→\]
    96→  2306→with $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ as in
    97→  2307→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}.  If $\mu$ has full support this
    98→  2308→reduces to~(a).
    99→  2309→\end{enumerate}
   100→  2310→\item Suppose $\mathbf R$ is compact metrizable or compact uniform,
   101→  2311→$\mathcal E$ is uniformly continuous and separated, the gap is evaluated in
   102→  2312→the worst-case/uniform sense, and $\mathcal E$ is \emph{uniformly compatible}
   103→  2313→with the uniformity of $\mathbf R$:
   104→  2314→\[
   105→  2315→\forall\,\text{entourages }U\ \exists\,\eta>0:\quad
   106→  2316→\max\{\mathcal E(X,Z),\mathcal E(Y,Z)\}<\eta
   107→  2317→\ \Longrightarrow\ (X,Y)\in U .
   108→  2318→\]
   109→  2319→Conversely, closeness in $\mathbf R$ implies small cost, by uniform continuity
   110→  2320→of $\mathcal E$.  On a compact metric space with
   111→  2321→$\mathcal E(X,Z)=0\iff X=Z$, joint continuity already implies this
   112→  2322→compatibility; stating it explicitly is what makes the passage from ``small
   113→  2323→cost to a common representative'' to ``close residual objects'' valid without
   114→  2324→a hidden asymmetry.  Then the following are equivalent:
   115→  2325→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
   116→  2326→\item $\delta$ is uniformly continuous for the profinite uniformity;
   117→  2327→\item $\delta$ extends continuously to $\widehat{\mathbf H}$;
   118→  2328→\item
   119→  2329→\[
   120→  2330→\lim_{M\to\infty}\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=0.
   121→  2331→\]
   122→  2332→\end{enumerate}
   123→  2333→\end{enumerate}
   124→  2334→\end{metatheorem}
   125→  2335→
   126→  2336→\begin{proof}
   127→  2337→Parts (i)--(iii) follow from the definitions.  Monotonicity follows because
   128→  2338→the feasible set of right congruences of index at most $M$ is nested:
   129→  2339→\[
   130→  2340→\{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M\}
   131→  2341→\subseteq
   132→  2342→\{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M+1\}.
   133→  2343→\]
   134→  2344→If $\sim_\delta$ has finite index, quotient by $\sim_\delta$ and assign each
   135→  2345→class its common residual $\Psi([u])=\delta(u)$, which is well defined since
   136→  2346→$u\sim_\delta v$ implies $\delta(u)=\delta(v)$.  Every history then incurs
   137→  2347→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=\mathcal E(\delta(u),\delta(u))=0$ by
   138→  2348→reflexivity, so the aggregated cost vanishes for any of the three aggregation
   139→  2349→schemes.  Reflexivity is exactly what this step consumes; without it the
   140→  2350→quotient reproduces the trajectory but need not reproduce it at zero cost.
   141→  2351→
   142→  2352→For the converse in~(iii), suppose $\mathcal E$ is separated and
   143→  2353→$\{0,\infty\}$-valued and the worst-case gap is zero at budget $M$.  Then
   144→  2354→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$ for every $u$, and separatedness upgrades
   145→  2355→this to $\delta(u)=\Psi([u])$.  Hence histories in a common class have equal
   146→  2356→residuals after every continuation, so the state relation refines
   147→  2357→$\sim_\delta$ and $\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M$.  Separatedness is
   148→  2358→what this direction consumes, and it is assumed in~(iii) but not in~(ii).
   149→  2359→
   150→  2360→For (iv), assume first that $\delta$ is uniformly continuous for the profinite
   151→  2361→uniformity.  By the universal property of completion, $\delta$ extends
   152→  2362→continuously to a map
   153→  2363→\[
   154→  2364→\widehat\delta:\widehat{\mathbf H}\to\mathbf R.
   155→  2365→\]
   156→  2366→Since $\widehat{\mathbf H}$ is compact and $\mathbf R$ is compact uniform,
   157→  2367→$\widehat\delta$ is uniformly continuous.  Hence for every cost tolerance
   158→  2368→$\varepsilon>0$ there is a finite clopen partition of $\widehat{\mathbf H}$,
   159→  2369→equivalently a finite-index right congruence $\sim$, such that
   160→  2370→\[
   161→  2371→u\sim v
   162→  2372→\implies
   163→  2373→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\delta(v))<\varepsilon
   164→  2374→\]
   165→  2375→in the uniform sense.  Choosing representatives for each $\sim$-class gives an
   166→  2376→$M$-state approximation with worst-case gap less than $\varepsilon$.
   167→  2377→
   168→  2378→Conversely, suppose $\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)\to0$ in the worst-case sense.  For
   169→  2379→every $\varepsilon>0$, choose a finite quotient $\sim$ and representative map
   170→  2380→$\Psi$ such that
   171→  2381→\[
   172→  2382→\sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
   173→  2383→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))
   174→  2384→<
   175→  2385→\eta,
   176→  2386→\]
   177→  2387→where $\eta>0$ is chosen small enough, using compactness and separatedness of
   178→  2388→$\mathcal E$, to ensure that whenever two histories $u,v$ are assigned the same
   179→  2389→representative, their residual objects are $\varepsilon$-close in the uniform
   180→  2390→structure of $\mathbf R$.  If $u\sim v$, then $\Psi([u])=\Psi([v])$, so
   181→  2391→$\delta(u)$ and $\delta(v)$ are $\varepsilon$-close.  Thus $\delta$ is
   182→  2392→uniformly continuous for the profinite uniformity and therefore extends
   183→  2393→continuously to $\widehat{\mathbf H}$.
   184→  2394→\end{proof}
   185→  2395→
   186→  2396→\begin{remark}[Average-case aggregation]
   187→  2397→\label{rem:average-profinite}
   188→  2398→Uniform worst-case approximation implies stationary average-case
   189→  2399→approximation.  The converse direction, from average-case gap convergence to
   190→  2400→profinite uniform continuity, requires additional observability or positivity
   191→  2401→assumptions on the stationary process.  The profinite equivalence is therefore
   192→  2402→stated for the worst-case/uniform gap unless such assumptions are supplied.
   193→  2403→\end{remark}

   196→  2406→\subsection{Boolean Zero-Error Dichotomy}
   197→  2407→\label{subsec:meta-boolean}

   199→  2409→
   200→  2410→\begin{definition}[Observable Support Index]
   201→  2411→\label{def:observable-support-index}
   202→  2412→Let $\mu$ be an average-case history law on $\mathcal I^*$ and let
   203→  2413→$S=\operatorname{supp}\mu$.  Such a $\mu$ exists for discounted-prefix
   204→  2414→aggregation, where
   205→  2415→$\mu(u)=(1-\beta)\beta^{|u|}\Pr[\text{prefix }u]$ is a probability measure on
   206→  2416→finite histories.  Stationary Ces\`aro aggregation does \emph{not} in general
   207→  2417→supply one: Definition~\ref{def:cesaro-agg} averages a cost process along a
   208→  2418→stationary trajectory and, when the process has infinite support, the Ces\`aro
   209→  2419→averages need not converge to the expectation under any single probability
   210→  2420→measure on $\mathcal I^*$.  The Ces\`aro case is treated separately in
   211→  2421→Remark~\ref{rem:cesaro-boolean}.
   212→  2422→The \textbf{observable support index} $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)$ is
   213→  2423→the minimum index of a right congruence $\sim$ on $\mathcal I^*$ for which
   214→  2424→there exists a representative map $\Psi$ with
   215→  2425→\[
   216→  2426→\mathcal E\bigl(\delta(u),\Psi([u])\bigr)=0
   217→  2427→\qquad
   218→  2428→\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }u .
   219→  2429→\]
   220→  2430→If $S$ is right-closed and $\mu(u)>0$ for every $u\in S$, define in addition
   221→  2431→the \emph{support-relative Nerode relation}
   222→  2432→\[
   223→  2433→u\sim_{\delta,S}v
   224→  2434→\iff
   225→  2435→\forall w\in\mathcal I^*\text{ with }uw,vw\in S:\quad
   226→  2436→\mathbf R\bigl(\delta(uw),\delta(vw)\bigr)
   227→  2437→=
   228→  2438→\mathbf R\bigl(\delta(vw),\delta(uw)\bigr)
   229→  2439→=
   230→  2440→0 .
   231→  2441→\]
   232→  2442→\end{definition}
   233→  2443→
   234→  2444→\begin{remark}[Restriction to the Support Is Not a Congruence]
   235→  2445→\label{rem:support-restriction-scope}
   236→  2446→The restriction $\sim_\delta\!\restriction_S$ is not in general a right
   237→  2447→congruence.  If $S$ is not closed under right extension, a pair $u,v\in S$ may
   238→  2448→have continuations $uw\notin S$ witnessing $u\not\sim_\delta v$ that the
   239→  2449→average cost never sees; and the restricted relation need not be compatible
   240→  2450→with the transition action.  The operative object is therefore the index of a
   241→  2451→global congruence that is observationally exact on $S$, which is what
   242→  2452→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index} names.  For right-closed $S$ the
   243→  2453→two indices are related by the sandwich of
   244→  2454→Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:boolean}(ii); they coincide precisely when the
   245→  2455→completion of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is index-preserving, and
   246→  2456→Remark~\ref{rem:support-extension-sharp} exhibits a right-closed support where
   247→  2457→they differ.
   248→  2458→\end{remark}
   249→  2459→
   250→  2460→\begin{lemma}[Index-Preserving Extension off the Support]
   251→  2461→\label{lem:support-extension}
   252→  2462→Let $S\subseteq\mathcal I^*$ be right-closed, i.e.\ $u\in S$ implies $uw\in S$
   253→  2463→for every $w\in\mathcal I^*$.  Let $\approx$ be an equivalence relation on $S$
   254→  2464→that is \emph{stable}: $u\approx v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$ imply $ux\approx vx$.
   255→  2465→Then there is a right congruence $\sim$ on all of $\mathcal I^*$ whose
   256→  2466→restriction to $S$ is $\approx$, and
   257→  2467→\[
   258→  2468→\operatorname{index}(\sim)
   259→  2469→=
   260→  2470→\operatorname{index}(\approx)+c_S ,
   261→  2471→\]
   262→  2472→where $c_S$ is the number of classes the construction requires off $S$.  One
   263→  2473→has $c_S=0$ when $S=\mathcal I^*$, and $c_S\le1$ whenever no history outside
   264→  2474→$S$ has an extension inside $S$.  In general $c_S$ may be infinite, in which
   265→  2475→case the construction yields a right congruence of infinite index and the
   266→  2476→upper bound is vacuous; the sandwich is informative exactly when the
   267→  2477→completion has finite index.
   268→  2478→\end{lemma}
   269→  2479→
   270→  2480→\begin{proof}
   271→  2481→Right-closure gives $u\in S\Rightarrow ux\in S$, so $S$ is forward invariant.
   272→  2482→The complement is \emph{not} forward invariant: a history outside $S$ may have
   273→  2483→an extension inside $S$.  For $S=\{u:u\text{ contains at least one }\mathtt b\}$,
   274→  2484→which is right-closed, one has $\mathtt{aaa}\notin S$ and $\mathtt{aaab}\in S$.
   275→  2485→The set $\{u:u\text{ ends in }\mathtt b\}$ would \emph{not} serve here, since
   276→  2486→appending $\mathtt a$ leaves it and it is therefore not right-closed.  The
   277→  2487→classes off $S$ therefore cannot be merged freely, and the construction below
   278→  2488→is stated explicitly.
   279→  2489→
   280→  2490→Define $\sim$ on $\mathcal I^*$ by declaring $u\sim v$ when either
   281→  2491→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
   282→  2492→\item $u,v\in S$ and $u\approx v$; or
   283→  2493→\item $u,v\notin S$ and for every $y\in\mathcal I^*$ one has
   284→  2494→$uy\in S\iff vy\in S$, and $uy\in S$ implies $uy\approx vy$.
   285→  2495→\end{enumerate}
   286→  2496→This is an equivalence relation, and no class meets both $S$ and its
   287→  2497→complement.
   288→  2498→
   289→  2499→\emph{Right congruence.}  Let $u\sim v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$.  If $u,v\in S$
   290→  2500→then $ux,vx\in S$ by right-closure and $ux\approx vx$ by stability, so
   291→  2501→$ux\sim vx$.  Suppose $u,v\notin S$.  For any $y\in\mathcal I^*$, applying~(b)
   292→  2502→to the continuation $xy$ gives $uxy\in S\iff vxy\in S$ and, when these hold,
   293→  2503→$uxy\approx vxy$.  If $ux\notin S$ then $vx\notin S$ by~(b) with $y=x$, and the
   294→  2504→displayed conditions are exactly~(b) for the pair $(ux,vx)$, so $ux\sim vx$.
   295→  2505→If $ux\in S$ then~(b) with $y=x$ gives $vx\in S$ and $ux\approx vx$, so
   296→  2506→$ux\sim vx$ by~(a).
   297→  2507→
   298→  2508→\emph{Index.}  The classes inside $S$ are exactly the $\approx$-classes, and
   299→  2509→those outside are the classes of~(b); writing $c_S$ for their number gives the
   300→  2510→stated count.  If no history outside $S$ has an extension inside $S$, then
   301→  2511→condition~(b) is vacuous and all such histories form a single class, so
   302→  2512→$c_S\le1$.
   303→  2513→\end{proof}
   304→  2514→
   305→  2515→\begin{remark}[The Extension Can Strictly Increase the Index]
   306→  2516→\label{rem:support-extension-sharp}
   307→  2517→The term $c_S$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is not an artifact of the
   308→  2518→construction: it can be strictly positive even for right-closed $S$, so the
   309→  2519→equality $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})$
   310→  2520→is false in general.
   311→  2521→
   312→  2522→Let $\mathcal I=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}$ and $S=\mathcal I^{+}$, the set of
   313→  2523→nonempty words, which is right-closed.  Let the residual take two values
   314→  2524→$A\ne B$ and set
   315→  2525→\[
   316→  2526→\delta(u)=
   317→  2527→\begin{cases}
   318→  2528→A, & u\text{ begins with }\mathtt a,\\
   319→  2529→B, & u\text{ begins with }\mathtt b,
   320→  2530→\end{cases}
   321→  2531→\qquad u\in S,
   322→  2532→\]
   323→  2533→with $\delta(\varepsilon)$ unconstrained since $\varepsilon\notin S$.  For
   324→  2534→$u,v\in S$ every continuation $uw,vw$ again lies in $S$ and has the residual
   325→  2535→determined by the first letter, so $u\sim_{\delta,S}v$ exactly when $u$ and $v$
   326→  2536→begin with the same letter:
   327→  2537→\[
   328→  2538→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})=2 .
   329→  2539→\]
   330→  2540→
   331→  2541→Now let $\sim$ be any right congruence on $\mathcal I^*$ admitting a
   332→  2542→representative map exact on $S$.  The class of $\varepsilon$ must send
   333→  2543→$\mathtt a$ into the $A$-class and $\mathtt b$ into the $B$-class.  But the
   334→  2544→$A$-class sends \emph{both} letters into the $A$-class, and the $B$-class sends
   335→  2545→both into the $B$-class, since $u\mathtt a$ and $u\mathtt b$ begin with the same
   336→  2546→letter as $u$.  So $\varepsilon$ has a transition pattern matching neither
   337→  2547→class and requires a third.  Hence
   338→  2548→\[
   339→  2549→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=3=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+1 ,
   340→  2550→\]
   341→  2551→attaining $c_S=1$.  A three-state witness is $q_\varepsilon$ with
   342→  2552→$\tau(q_\varepsilon,\mathtt a)=q_A$, $\tau(q_\varepsilon,\mathtt b)=q_B$ and
   343→  2553→$q_A,q_B$ absorbing.
   344→  2554→
   345→  2555→Two consequences follow.  First, the sandwich of
   346→  2556→Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:boolean}(ii) is sharp at both ends.  Second, the
   347→  2557→obstruction requires $\mu(\varepsilon)=0$: if $\mu$ is a discounted-prefix law
   348→  2558→with $\mu(\varepsilon)>0$, then $\varepsilon\in S$, and right-closure forces
   349→  2559→$S=\mathcal I^*$, whence $c_S=0$ and the equality does hold.
   350→  2560→\end{remark}
   351→  2561→
   352→  2562→\begin{metatheorem}[Boolean Zero-Error Dichotomy]
   353→  2563→\label{meta:boolean}
   354→  2564→Let $\mathbb T$ be separated with $\mathcal E$ $\{0,\infty\}$-valued.
   355→  2565→
   356→  2566→\emph{(i) Worst-case aggregation.}  If
   357→  2567→\[
   358→  2568→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)
   359→  2569→=
   360→  2570→\inf_{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M}
   361→  2571→\ \sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
   362→  2572→\mathcal E\bigl(\delta(u),\Psi([u])\bigr),
   363→  2573→\]
   364→  2574→then
   365→  2575→\[
   366→  2576→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)=0
   367→  2577→\iff
   368→  2578→\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M.
   369→  2579→\]
   370→  2580→
   371→  2581→\emph{(ii) Average-case aggregation.}  If the aggregation is discounted-prefix
   372→  2582→with history law $\mu$ on $\mathcal I^*$, then
   373→  2583→\[
   374→  2584→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0
   375→  2585→\iff
   376→  2586→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M,
   377→  2587→\]
   378→  2588→with $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observable support index of
   379→  2589→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}.  If $\mu$ has full support on
   380→  2590→$\mathcal I^*$ then
   381→  2591→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)$ and~(ii)
   382→  2592→reduces to~(i).  If $S=\operatorname{supp}\mu$ is right-closed with positive
   383→  2593→mass, then
   384→  2594→\[
   385→  2595→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})
   386→  2596→\ \le\
   387→  2597→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)
   388→  2598→\ \le\
   389→  2599→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+c_S,
   390→  2600→\]
   391→  2601→where $c_S$ is the number of additional classes required off $S$ by
   392→  2602→Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}; in particular $c_S=0$ when
   393→  2603→$S=\mathcal I^*$, and equality
   394→  2604→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})$ holds
   395→  2605→whenever the extension of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is
   396→  2606→index-preserving.
   397→  2607→In general the average-case index may be strictly smaller than the worst-case
   398→  2608→index, since a machine may err on $\mu$-null histories at no cost.
   399→  2609→\end{metatheorem}
   400→  2610→
   401→  2611→\begin{remark}[The Ces\`aro Case]
   402→  2612→\label{rem:cesaro-boolean}
   403→  2613→Clause~(ii) is stated for discounted-prefix aggregation because that scheme
   404→  2614→carries a genuine probability measure on finite histories.  Stationary
   405→  2615→Ces\`aro aggregation does not, so the phrase ``$\mu$-almost every history'' has
   406→  2616→no direct meaning there and the clause must be reformulated.
   407→  2617→
   408→  2618→Two formulations are available.  On the \emph{stationary state space}, when
   409→  2619→the process is a finite-state stationary ergodic chain with positive-mass
   410→  2620→state set $\Splus$, the natural statement replaces histories by states: zero
   411→  2621→Ces\`aro cost holds at budget $M$ exactly when some right congruence of index
   412→  2622→at most $M$ is exact on $\Splus$, which is
   413→  2623→Theorem~\ref{thm:retention-zero} in the $\{0,\infty\}$-valued case.  On
   414→  2624→\emph{histories}, the correct surrogate for ``$\mu$-almost every'' is
   415→  2625→\emph{zero asymptotic frequency of error}: the Ces\`aro cost vanishes exactly
   416→  2626→when
   417→  2627→\[
   418→  2628→\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\,\bigl|\{t<T:\ \mathcal E(\delta(U_t),\Psi([U_t]))>0\}\bigr|
   419→  2629→=0
   420→  2630→\]
   421→  2631→almost surely.  For a finite-state ergodic chain the two agree, since a state
   422→  2632→of positive stationary mass is visited with positive asymptotic frequency.
   423→  2633→Outside that setting the second is the operative condition, and no formula in
   424→  2634→terms of a support-relative Nerode relation is claimed.
   425→  2635→\end{remark}
   426→  2636→
   427→  2637→\begin{proof}
   428→  2638→\emph{(i)} If $\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M$, quotienting by
   429→  2639→$\sim_\delta$ and assigning each class its common residual gives an $M$-state
   430→  2640→approximation with zero cost at every history.  Conversely, suppose an
   431→  2641→$M$-state approximation has zero worst-case cost.  Then for \emph{every}
   432→  2642→history $u$ we have $\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$, so by separatedness
   433→  2643→$\delta(u)=\Psi([u])$.  Hence any two histories in the same machine state have
   434→  2644→equal residuals, and, the state relation being a right congruence, equal
   435→  2645→residuals after every continuation; that is, the state relation refines
   436→  2646→$\sim_\delta$.  Therefore
   437→  2647→$\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M$.
   438→  2648→
   439→  2649→\emph{(ii)} Suppose $\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0$.  Then there are a right
   440→  2650→congruence $\sim$ of index at most $M$ and a map $\Psi$ with
   441→  2651→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$ for $\mu$-almost every $u$.  That is
   442→  2652→precisely the defining property in
   443→  2653→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}, so
   444→  2654→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M$.
   445→  2655→
   446→  2656→Conversely, if $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M$, take a witnessing
   447→  2657→congruence and representative map; the induced $M$-state machine has zero
   448→  2658→$\mu$-average cost by construction.
   449→  2659→
   450→  2660→If $\mu$ has full support, ``$\mu$-almost every'' is ``every'', and the
   451→  2661→argument of~(i) applies verbatim, giving
   452→  2662→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)$.
   453→  2663→
   454→  2664→If $S$ is right-closed with positive mass, then for $u,v\in S$ the constraint
   455→  2665→is exactly that all continuations \emph{within $S$} agree, which is
   456→  2666→$\sim_{\delta,S}$.  Right-closure makes $\sim_{\delta,S}$ stable under the
   457→  2667→transition action in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}: if
   458→  2668→$u\sim_{\delta,S}v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$, then for every $w$ with
   459→  2669→$uxw,vxw\in S$ the defining condition applied to the continuation $xw$ gives
   460→  2670→$\delta(uxw)=\delta(vxw)$, so $ux\sim_{\delta,S}vx$.
   461→  2671→
   462→  2672→For the lower bound, any global right congruence $\sim$ witnessing
   463→  2673→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ must, restricted to $S$, refine $\sim_{\delta,S}$: two
   464→  2674→$\sim$-equivalent histories in $S$ receive the same representative and hence
   465→  2675→the same residual after every continuation remaining in $S$.  Therefore
   466→  2676→$\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})\le\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)$.
   467→  2677→
   468→  2678→For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} to
   469→  2679→$\approx\,=\,\sim_{\delta,S}$ to obtain a global right congruence restricting
   470→  2680→to it on $S$, of index $\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+c_S$; assigning
   471→  2681→each class inside $S$ its common residual gives zero cost $\mu$-almost
   472→  2682→everywhere.  This is the stated sandwich.
   473→  2683→
   474→  2684→The extension step is not vacuous: a history outside $S$ may have an extension
   475→  2685→inside $S$, so the classes off $S$ cannot be merged arbitrarily without
   476→  2686→destroying the right-congruence property.  Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}
   477→  2687→supplies the correct construction, and $c_S=0$ exactly when it is
   478→  2688→index-preserving.
   479→  2689→\end{proof}
   480→  2690→
   481→  2691→\begin{remark}[Why the Qualifier Is Necessary]
   482→  2692→\label{rem:boolean-aggregation-qualifier}
   483→  2693→The two clauses differ.  Let $\delta$ be a residual trajectory
   484→  2694→requiring $\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)=k$ on all of $\mathcal I^*$, but
   485→  2695→suppose the input process never visits a set of histories whose removal
   486→  2696→collapses $\sim_\delta$ to $k'<k$ classes.  Then a $k'$-state machine has zero
   487→  2697→average cost while failing to realize $\delta$ exactly, so the unqualified
   488→  2698→biconditional would be false.  Statements elsewhere in this manuscript that
   489→  2699→invoke ``zero error'' should be read with clause~(i) unless a stationary
   490→  2700→support is explicitly named, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:retention-zero}, which is
   491→  2701→a clause~(ii) statement with $\operatorname{supp}\mu=\Splus$, so the
   492→  2702→relevant quantity there is $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ rather than the restriction
   493→  2703→of $\sim_\delta$ to the support.
   494→  2704→\end{remark}

   496→  2706→\begin{corollary}[Normalized $\{0,1\}$ Commitment Cost]

   499→  2709→\[
   500→  2710→\mathcal E(f,g)=
   501→  2711→\begin{cases}
   502→  2712→0,&f=g,\\
   503→  2713→1,&f\neq g,

   506→  2716→and the aggregation is worst-case, then

   521→  2731→\subsection{Exact Spectral Converse for Linear Hankel Gaps}
   522→  2732→\label{subsec:meta-spectral}
   523→  2733→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   524→  2734→
   525→  2735→\begin{metatheorem}[Eckart--Young--Mirsky Spectral Converse]
   526→  2736→\label{meta:spectral}
   527→  2737→Let $\mathbb T$ be a Hilbert-module task theory with compact Hankel operator
   528→  2738→$H_\delta$.  Define the unrestricted linear rank-$M$ gap by
   529→  2739→\[
   530→  2740→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{unres}}(M)
   531→  2741→=
   532→  2742→\inf_{\operatorname{rank}B\le M}
   533→  2743→\norm{H_\delta-B}_{\mathrm{op}}.
   534→  2744→\]
   535→  2745→Then
   536→  2746→\[
   537→  2747→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{unres}}(M)
   538→  2748→=
   539→  2749→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta).
   540→  2750→\]
   541→  2751→Define the Hankel-structured finite-rank gap by
   542→  2752→\[
   543→  2753→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{Hank,str}}(M)
   544→  2754→=
   545→  2755→\inf_{\substack{
   546→  2756→\operatorname{rank}B\le M\\
   547→  2757→B\ \text{Hankel}
   548→  2758→}}
   549→  2759→\norm{H_\delta-B}_{\mathrm{op}}.
   550→  2760→\]
   551→  2761→Then
   552→  2762→\[
   553→  2763→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{Hank,str}}(M)
   554→  2764→\ge
   555→  2765→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta),
   556→  2766→\]
   557→  2767→with equality if an optimal rank-$M$ truncation can be chosen Hankel, or under
   558→  2768→a valid Hardy-space Adamjan--Arov--Krein embedding satisfying its hypotheses.
   559→  2769→\end{metatheorem}
   560→  2770→
   561→  2771→\begin{proof}
   562→  2772→The unrestricted equality is the Eckart--Young--Mirsky theorem for compact
   563→  2773→operators on Hilbert space:
   564→  2774→\[
   565→  2775→\inf_{\operatorname{rank}B\le M}
   566→  2776→\norm{H_\delta-B}_{\mathrm{op}}
   567→  2777→=
   568→  2778→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta).
   569→  2779→\]
   570→  2780→The Hankel-structured feasible set is a subset of the unrestricted feasible
   571→  2781→set, so restricting the infimum cannot decrease the value.  Hence
   572→  2782→\[
   573→  2783→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{Hank,str}}(M)
   574→  2784→\ge
   575→  2785→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mathrm{unres}}(M)
   576→  2786→=
   577→  2787→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta).
   578→  2788→\]
   579→  2789→Equality requires that an unrestricted optimal rank-$M$ approximant can be
   580→  2790→chosen Hankel, or that a separate Hankel approximation theorem applies.
   581→  2791→\end{proof}
   582→  2792→
   583→  2793→\begin{remark}[AAK versus EYM]
   584→  2794→\label{rem:aak-eym-meta}
   585→  2795→The theorem above uses Eckart--Young--Mirsky \cite{eckart1936,mirsky1960}.  As
   586→  2796→already noted in Remark~\ref{rem:aak-eym-hilbert} for the Hilbert-module
   587→  2797→instantiation, the Adamjan--Arov--Krein theorem \cite{aak1971} applies only
   588→  2798→after a Hardy-space embedding has been separately established, and the model
   589→  2799→does not assume one by default.
   590→  2800→\end{remark}
   591→  2801→
   592→  2802→\begin{corollary}[Stable Residual-Norm Spectral Bound]
   593→  2803→\label{cor:stable-residual-spectral}
   594→  2804→Suppose a residual-cost functional satisfies
   595→  2805→\[
   596→  2806→\norm{h_\delta-h_{\widehat\delta}}_{\infty,\rho}
   597→  2807→\le
   598→  2808→\mathcal L_{\infty,\rho}(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   599→  2809→\]
   600→  2810→with $0<\rho<1/|\Sigma|$.  Then for every linear $M$-rank approximation
   601→  2811→$\widehat\delta$ with $\operatorname{rank}H_{\widehat\delta}\le M$,
   602→  2812→\[
   603→  2813→\mathcal L_{\infty,\rho}(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   604→  2814→\ge
   605→  2815→(1-|\Sigma|\rho)
   606→  2816→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta).
   607→  2817→\]
   608→  2818→\end{corollary}
   609→  2819→
   610→  2820→\begin{proof}
   611→  2821→By Proposition~\ref{prop:stable-decay},
   612→  2822→\[
   613→  2823→\norm{H_\delta-H_{\widehat\delta}}_{\mathrm{op}}
   614→  2824→\le
   615→  2825→\frac{1}{1-|\Sigma|\rho}
   616→  2826→\norm{h_\delta-h_{\widehat\delta}}_{\infty,\rho}.
   617→  2827→\]
   618→  2828→By Eckart--Young--Mirsky, since
   619→  2829→$\operatorname{rank}H_{\widehat\delta}\le M$,
   620→  2830→\[
   621→  2831→\norm{H_\delta-H_{\widehat\delta}}_{\mathrm{op}}
   622→  2832→\ge
   623→  2833→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta).
   624→  2834→\]
   625→  2835→Combining the two inequalities gives
   626→  2836→\[
   627→  2837→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta)
   628→  2838→\le
   629→  2839→\frac{1}{1-|\Sigma|\rho}
   630→  2840→\norm{h_\delta-h_{\widehat\delta}}_{\infty,\rho}.
   631→  2841→\]
   632→  2842→Using the assumed domination of the weighted residual norm by the cost gives
   633→  2843→\[
   634→  2844→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\delta)
   635→  2845→\le
   636→  2846→\frac{1}{1-|\Sigma|\rho}
   637→  2847→\mathcal L_{\infty,\rho}(\delta,\widehat\delta),
   638→  2848→\]
   639→  2849→which rearranges to the stated bound.
   640→  2850→\end{proof}
   641→  2851→
   642→  2852→This corollary is a domination-bridge result.  It does not assert that the
   643→  2853→residual-cost linear finite-rank gap equals the Hankel spectral tail unless
   644→  2854→the task cost is exactly the relevant operator or weighted residual norm and
   645→  2855→the feasible set is unrestricted.

   648→  2858→\subsection{Information-Bottleneck Meta-Theorem for Full KL}
   649→  2859→\label{subsec:meta-ib}
   650→  2860→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   651→  2861→
   652→  2862→\begin{metatheorem}[Finite-State Information Bottleneck]
   653→  2863→\label{meta:ib}
   654→  2864→Let $S=S_t$, let $K=K_t=\phi(S_t)$ be a lumpable quotient, and let $Z=Z_t$ be
   655→  2865→the predictive future, by default the next output.  For every lumpable quotient
   656→  2866→$\phi$,
   657→  2867→\[
   658→  2868→\RetKL(\phi)
   659→  2869→=
   660→  2870→I(S;Z\mid K).
   661→  2871→\]
   662→  2872→Consequently,
   663→  2873→\[
   664→  2874→\RetKL(\phi)
   665→  2875→=
   666→  2876→I(S;Z)-I(K;Z).
   667→  2877→\]
   668→  2878→Therefore,
   669→  2879→\[
   670→  2880→\RetKL(M)
   671→  2881→=
   672→  2882→I(S;Z)
   673→  2883→-
   674→  2884→\max_{\substack{
   675→  2885→\phi\ \text{lumpable}\\
   676→  2886→|\mathcal K|\le M
   677→  2887→}}
   678→  2888→I(K;Z).
   679→  2889→\]
   680→  2890→\end{metatheorem}
   681→  2891→
   682→  2892→\begin{proof}
   683→  2893→Since $K=\phi(S)$, $K$ is a function of $S$.  Since $S$ is sufficient for $Z$,
   684→  2894→\[
   685→  2895→Z\perp\!\!\!\perp K\mid S.
   686→  2896→\]
   687→  2897→Therefore,
   688→  2898→\[
   689→  2899→I(K;Z\mid S)=0.
   690→  2900→\]
   691→  2901→For a fixed quotient $K$, the optimal representative in each block $k$ is the
   692→  2902→conditional law $P_{Z\mid K=k}$, which equals the mixture centroid
   693→  2903→$\bar P_k$.  Hence
   694→  2904→\[
   695→  2905→\RetKL(\phi)
   696→  2906→=
   697→  2907→\mathbb E
   698→  2908→D_{\mathrm{KL}}
   699→  2909→\bigl(
   700→  2910→P_{Z\mid S}
   701→  2911→\|
   702→  2912→P_{Z\mid K}
   703→  2913→\bigr)
   704→  2914→=
   705→  2915→I(S;Z\mid K).
   706→  2916→\]
   707→  2917→By the chain rule,
   708→  2918→\[
   709→  2919→I(S,K;Z)
   710→  2920→=
   711→  2921→I(K;Z)+I(S;Z\mid K).
   712→  2922→\]
   713→  2923→But $K$ is a function of $S$, so
   714→  2924→\[
   715→  2925→I(S,K;Z)=I(S;Z).
   716→  2926→\]
   717→  2927→The first identity $\RetKL(\phi)=I(S;Z\mid K)$ holds in $[0,\infty]$ without
   718→  2928→restriction, regular conditional probabilities existing because $Z$ is standard
   719→  2929→Borel.  The subtracted form
   720→  2930→\[
   721→  2931→I(S;Z\mid K)
   722→  2932→=
   723→  2933→I(S;Z)-I(K;Z)
   724→  2934→\]
   725→  2935→requires $I(S;Z)<\infty$, since otherwise the right-hand side is the undefined
   726→  2936→difference $\infty-\infty$.  In the present finite-state setting that condition
   727→  2937→is automatic: $S$ takes finitely many values, so
   728→  2938→\[
   729→  2939→I(S;Z)\le H(S)<\infty ,
   730→  2940→\]
   731→  2941→and $I(K;Z)\le I(S;Z)<\infty$ by data processing, so the rearrangement is
   732→  2942→always legitimate here.  The statement carries the hypothesis explicitly
   733→  2943→because the identity is applied in the sequel to state sets that are finite by
   734→  2944→assumption rather than by construction.  All variational
   735→  2945→statements below are to be read in the conditional-mutual-information form,
   736→  2946→which is unconditionally defined.
   737→  2947→Minimizing over lumpable quotients of size at most $M$ is equivalent to
   738→  2948→maximizing $I(K;Z)$ over the same feasible set.
   739→  2949→\end{proof}
   740→  2950→
   741→  2951→The identity is stated for the input-marginal target; its controlled analogue,
   742→  2952→conditional on the input and valid for general unifilar machines, is
   743→  2953→Theorem~\ref{thm:controlled-ib}.
   744→  2954→
   745→  2955→\begin{corollary}[Lumpable Partition Form]
   746→  2956→\label{cor:ib-lumpable}
   747→  2957→If $\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K$ is a lumpable quotient with blocks
   748→  2958→$C_k=\{s\in\Splus:\phi(s)=k\}$, then
   749→  2959→\[
   750→  2960→\RetKL(M)
   751→  2961→=
   752→  2962→\min_{\substack{
   753→  2963→\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K\ \text{lumpable}\\
   754→  2964→|\mathcal K|\le M
   755→  2965→}}
   756→  2966→\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
   757→  2967→\sum_{s\in C_k}
   758→  2968→\pi_s
   759→  2969→D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_s\|\bar P_k),
   760→  2970→\]
   761→  2971→where
   762→  2972→\[
   763→  2973→\bar P_k
   764→  2974→=
   765→  2975→\frac{
   766→  2976→\sum_{s\in C_k}\pi_s P_s
   767→  2977→}{
   768→  2978→\sum_{s\in C_k}\pi_s
   769→  2979→}.
   770→  2980→\]
   771→  2981→\end{corollary}

   794→  3004→\label{subsec:typed-principle}
   795→  3005→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   796→  3006→
   797→  3007→\begin{metatheorem}[Typed Finite-State Rate--Distortion Principle]
   798→  3008→\label{meta:typed-rate-distortion}
   799→  3009→Fix a regime $\mathsf r$ specified by a rooted history right action
   800→  3010→$H_{\mathsf r}$ over an event alphabet $\mathcal A_{\mathsf r}$, a residual map
   801→  3011→$\delta_{\mathsf r}:H_{\mathsf r}\to\mathbf R_{\mathsf r}$, a reflexive cost
   802→  3012→$\mathcal E_{\mathsf r}$, a declared aggregation and support, and a class
   803→  3013→$\mathfrak Q_{\mathsf r}(M)$ of admissible finite-state factors of index at
   804→  3014→most $M$, with
   805→  3015→\[
   806→  3016→\Delta_{\mathsf r}(M)
   807→  3017→=
   808→  3018→\inf_{(Q,\Psi)\in\mathfrak Q_{\mathsf r}(M)}
   809→  3019→\mathcal L_{\mathsf r}\bigl(\delta_{\mathsf r},\Psi\circ Q\bigr).
   810→  3020→\]
   811→  3021→Then:
   812→  3022→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
   813→  3023→\item $\Delta_{\mathsf r}(M)$ is nonincreasing in $M$
   814→  3024→(Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:monotone}(i));
   815→  3025→\item exact zero distortion is governed by the least admissible factor
   816→  3026→through which $\delta_{\mathsf r}$ factors on the declared support: for
   817→  3027→separated $\{0,\infty\}$-valued costs this is
   818→  3028→Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:monotone}(iii), with the index taken relative to the
   819→  3029→support in the average-aggregation case;
   820→  3030→\item every quantitative converse beyond~(b) requires a regime-specific
   821→  3031→analytical bridge; no such bound follows from the factorization syntax alone;
   822→  3032→\item if the regime admits a Schatten-$p$ response representation with
   823→  3033→response operator $A_{\delta_{\mathsf r}}$, effective rank budget
   824→  3034→$r_{\mathsf r}(M)$ and modulus $c_{\mathsf r}$, then
   825→  3035→\[
   826→  3036→\Delta_{\mathsf r}(M)
   827→  3037→\ \ge\

   870→  3080→\begin{remark}[What the Principle Does and Does Not Unify]
   871→  3081→\label{rem:typed-principle-scope}
   872→  3082→The principle is a statement about shared \emph{syntax} together with an
   873→  3083→explicit refusal to share \emph{analysis}.  Clauses~(a) and~(b) are genuinely
   874→  3084→regime-independent: monotonicity and the zero-distortion threshold follow from
   875→  3085→the factorization structure alone.  Clause~(d) is conditional on a
   876→  3086→representation being supplied, and the three regimes supply different operators
   877→  3087→--- $H_\nu$, $\Sigma_\pi$, $N_\Lambda$ --- with different effective rank
   878→  3088→budgets $M$, $M-1$, $M$ and unrelated moduli, which is precisely the content of
   879→  3089→clause~(e).
   880→  3090→
   881→  3091→Two things the principle deliberately does not assert.  It does not claim a
   882→  3092→common history alphabet: commitment acts on $\mathcal I^*$ while retention in
   883→  3093→the unifilar generality of Definition~\ref{def:unifilar-machine} and grounding
   884→  3094→act on $(\mathcal I\times\mathcal O)^*$, and the input-driven retention model
   885→  3095→of Definition~\ref{def:controlled-markov} is the output-independent case in
   886→  3096→which the action factors through the input projection
   887→  3097→(Remark~\ref{rem:unifilar-proper-subclass}).  And it does not assert a
   888→  3098→per-symbol rate: the resource is the quotient index, whose description length
   889→  3099→is $R(M)=\log M$ by Definition~\ref{def:state-rate}.
   890→  3100→\end{remark}
