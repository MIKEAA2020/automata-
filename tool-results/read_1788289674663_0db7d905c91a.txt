  2001→\phi(\sigma(vx)),
  2002→\]
  2003→so $ux\sim_\phi vx$.  Hence $\sim_\phi$ is a right congruence on long
  2004→histories.
  2005→
  2006→Conversely, let $\sim$ be a finite-index right congruence coarser than
  2007→$\sim_Z$, and assume $\sim_Z$ is a right congruence.  Each $\sim$-class is then
  2008→a union of $\sim_Z$-classes.  Define $\phi(s)$ to be the $\sim$-class of any
  2009→long history $u$ with $\sigma(u)=s$.  This is well defined: two long histories
  2010→with the same $\sim_Z$-state are $\sim_Z$-equivalent, hence $\sim$-equivalent
  2011→because $\sim$ is coarser.  The right-congruence property gives
  2012→\[
  2013→\phi(\tau(s,x))=\tau_{\mathcal K}(\phi(s),x),
  2014→\]
  2015→so $\phi$ is lumpable.
  2016→\end{proof}
  2017→
  2018→\begin{example}[One-Step Predictive Equivalence Need Not Be a Congruence]
  2019→\label{ex:onestep-not-congruence}
  2020→Let $\mathcal S=\{s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3\}$ with a single input letter $a$,
  2021→$\mathcal O=\{0,1\}$, and next-output laws
  2022→\[
  2023→P_{s_0}=P_{s_1}=\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\tfrac12),
  2024→\qquad
  2025→P_{s_2}=\delta_0,
  2026→\qquad
  2027→P_{s_3}=\delta_1 .
  2028→\]
  2029→Let the transitions be
  2030→\[
  2031→\tau(s_0,a)=s_2,\quad
  2032→\tau(s_1,a)=s_3,\quad
  2033→\tau(s_2,a)=s_2,\quad
  2034→\tau(s_3,a)=s_3 .
  2035→\]
  2036→Taking $Z=Y$, the next output, gives $s_0\sim_Ys_1$, since both are fair
  2037→coins.  But
  2038→\[
  2039→\tau(s_0,a)=s_2\not\sim_Y s_3=\tau(s_1,a),
  2040→\]
  2041→so $\sim_Y$ is not a right congruence, and the partition
  2042→$\{\{s_0,s_1\},\{s_2\},\{s_3\}\}$ is not lumpable: the block
  2043→$\{s_0,s_1\}$ has no well-defined successor block.
  2044→
  2045→Taking instead $Z$ to be the full future separates $s_0$ from $s_1$, because
  2046→their continuations are the constant sequences $0^\infty$ and $1^\infty$.  With
  2047→that choice $\sim_Z$ is causal-state equivalence and the converse of
  2048→Proposition~\ref{prop:lumpability} applies.
  2049→
  2050→Since input-driven machines are unifilar
  2051→(Remark~\ref{rem:unifilar-proper-subclass}), the same example shows that
  2052→one-step predictive equivalence need not be a support-relative right
  2053→congruence on joint histories in the general model.
  2054→\end{example}

  2057→\subsection{Full-KL Retention Gap}
  2058→\label{subsec:full-kl-gap}
  2059→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2060→
  2061→\begin{definition}[Full-KL Retention Gap]
  2062→\label{def:full-kl-retention}
  2063→For a stationary controlled causal machine, define
  2064→\[
  2065→\RetKL(M)
  2066→=
  2067→\min_{\substack{
  2068→\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K\ \text{lumpable}\\
  2069→|\mathcal K|\le M
  2070→}}
  2071→\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
  2072→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}
  2073→\pi_s
  2074→D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_s\|\bar P_k),
  2075→\]
  2076→where
  2077→\[
  2078→\bar P_k
  2079→=
  2080→\frac{
  2081→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s P_s
  2082→}{
  2083→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s
  2084→}.
  2085→\]
  2086→Blocks of zero stationary mass are ignored.  Because $\Splus$ is finite, the
  2087→minimum exists.
  2088→
  2089→For unifilar machines with input-dependent emissions the operative object is
  2090→the controlled gap of Definition~\ref{def:controlled-full-kl}, of which the
  2091→present definition is the output-independent specialization
  2092→(Corollary~\ref{cor:controlled-reduces}).
  2093→\end{definition}
  2094→
  2095→In a synchronized machine, lumpable quotients of $\Splus$ correspond to
  2096→finite-index right congruences on sufficiently long histories by
  2097→Proposition~\ref{prop:lumpability}.  The full-KL centroid $\bar P_k$ is a
  2098→mixture distribution.  It is not, in general, an arithmetic mean of
  2099→parameters.  The arithmetic-mean centroid appears only in restricted
  2100→parametric families, such as the Gaussian quadratic restricted regime.
  2101→

  2103→\subsection{Gaussian Quadratic Restricted Retention Gap}
  2104→\label{subsec:gaussian-quadratic}
  2105→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2106→
  2107→\begin{definition}[Gaussian Quadratic Restricted Retention]
  2108→\label{def:gaussian-quadratic}
  2109→A stationary controlled causal machine is in the \textbf{Gaussian quadratic
  2110→restricted regime} if:
  2111→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  2112→\item the output space is the standard Borel space $\mathcal O=\mathbb R^D$;
  2113→\item each positive-mass state $s\in\Splus$ has predictive distribution
  2114→\[
  2115→P_s=\mathcal N(m_s,\tfrac12 I_D);
  2116→\]
  2117→\item admissible representatives are restricted to the same fixed-covariance
  2118→Gaussian family:
  2119→\[
  2120→Q_c=\mathcal N(c,\tfrac12 I_D);
  2121→\]
  2122→\item the cost is the KL divergence restricted to this family.
  2123→\end{enumerate}
  2124→For this family,
  2125→\[
  2126→D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_s\|Q_c)
  2127→=
  2128→\norm{m_s-c}^2.
  2129→\]
  2130→Equivalently, define Fisher-scaled features
  2131→\[
  2132→y_s=\sqrt{2}\,m_s,
  2133→\qquad
  2134→c_y=\sqrt{2}\,c.
  2135→\]
  2136→Then
  2137→\[
  2138→D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_s\|Q_c)
  2139→=
  2140→\frac12
  2141→\norm{y_s-c_y}^2.
  2142→\]
  2143→The associated quadratic gap is
  2144→\[
  2145→\RetQuad(M)
  2146→=
  2147→\min_{\substack{
  2148→\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K\ \text{lumpable}\\
  2149→|\mathcal K|\le M
  2150→}}
  2151→\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
  2152→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}
  2153→\pi_s
  2154→\norm{m_s-\bar m_k}^2,
  2155→\]
  2156→where
  2157→\[
  2158→\bar m_k
  2159→=
  2160→\frac{
  2161→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s m_s
  2162→}{
  2163→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s
  2164→}.
  2165→\]
  2166→Equivalently, in Fisher-scaled coordinates,
  2167→\[
  2168→\RetQuad(M)
  2169→=
  2170→\min_{\substack{
  2171→\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K\ \text{lumpable}\\
  2172→|\mathcal K|\le M
  2173→}}
  2174→\frac12
  2175→\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
  2176→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}
  2177→\pi_s
  2178→\norm{y_s-\bar y_k}^2,
  2179→\]
  2180→where
  2181→\[
  2182→\bar y_k
  2183→=
  2184→\frac{
  2185→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s y_s
  2186→}{
  2187→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s
  2188→}.
  2189→\]
  2190→Blocks of zero stationary mass are ignored.
  2191→\end{definition}
  2192→
  2193→The restricted Gaussian centroid is the arithmetic mean, either in mean
  2194→coordinates $m_s$ or in Fisher-scaled coordinates $y_s$.  The unrestricted
  2195→full-KL centroid is the mixture distribution $\bar P_k$.  These are not the
  2196→same.
  2197→
  2198→The quadratic gap is a weighted $k$-means objective with a lumpability
  2199→constraint.  It is the object used for the spectral retention converse and for
  2200→the Gaussian quadratic NP-hardness theorem.  It is not the full-KL retention
  2201→gap unless the full-KL problem has been explicitly restricted to the
  2202→fixed-covariance Gaussian family.

  2206→\label{sec:meta}
  2207→%======================================================================
  2208→
  2209→The schema supports several structural meta-theorems.  These results are
  2210→governed by finite-index right congruences, quotient factorization, and
  2211→separated cost functionals.  Their proofs remain regime-specific:
  2212→Myhill--Nerode theory, operator approximation, and information processing

  2215→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2216→\subsection{Master Scope: A Typed Variational Picture}
  2217→\label{subsec:meta-scope}
  2218→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2219→
  2220→The results of this manuscript, across commitment, retention, and grounding,
  2221→instantiate one typed variational picture along four pillars.  Stating them
  2222→together fixes what is unified and what is not; this subsection is a summary
  2223→of scope, not a new derivation.
  2224→
  2225→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
  2226→\item \textbf{Typed variational schema.}
  2227→Every regime gap has the shape
  2228→$\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=\inf_{\text{budget-}M\text{ feasible }B}
  2229→\mathcal L(\delta,B)$,
  2230→but the feasible class carries a regime-specific type: a finite right
  2231→congruence on histories for commitment (Section~\ref{subsec:det-spec}), a
  2232→stationary lumpable quotient for retention
  2233→(Section~\ref{subsec:shared-schema-intro}), a deterministic Mealy machine for
  2234→the symbolic grounding gap (Definition~\ref{def:symbolic-grounding-gap}), and
  2235→a finite-rank bounded operator for the unrestricted linear grounding
  2236→relaxation (Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-grounding}).  The budget $M$ is a
  2237→right-congruence index in the first three cases and an operator rank in the
  2238→fourth; these are not asserted equivalent
  2239→(Remark~\ref{rem:quotient-display-scope}).
  2240→
  2241→\item \textbf{Structural monotonicity and zero thresholds.}
  2242→Each regime gap is non-increasing in $M$ and has an exact zero threshold on
  2243→its own feasible class: the Myhill--Nerode index for commitment
  2244→(Theorem~\ref{thm:commitment-spec}), the stationary support size of distinct
  2245→predictive laws for full-KL retention (Theorem~\ref{thm:retention-zero}), and
  2246→the operator rank of $H_\nu$ for the linear grounding relaxations
  2247→(Proposition~\ref{prop:grounding-structured-zero}).  These thresholds are
  2248→proved separately in each regime; no single argument produces all three.
  2249→
  2250→\item \textbf{Conditional response-operator converse.}
  2251→Whenever a regime's approximants admit a response operator of bounded rank
  2252→dominating the task cost (Definition~\ref{def:response}), the unified
  2253→Schatten converse of Theorem~\ref{thm:unified} supplies a spectral-tail lower
  2254→bound.  This bridge is conditional on the domination hypothesis and on the
  2255→domination modulus, which may itself depend on the budget
  2256→(Remark~\ref{rem:bridge-consequence}); it does not by itself make the three
  2257→regimes' numerical gaps comparable (Theorem~\ref{thm:schatten-nogo}).
  2258→
  2259→\item \textbf{Regime-specific analytical engines.}
  2260→The zero thresholds and quantitative rates are proved by different classical
  2261→theories: Myhill--Nerode automata theory and minimax game values for
  2262→commitment, information-bottleneck and Fisher-information asymptotics for
  2263→retention, and Eckart--Young--Mirsky/Adamjan--Arov--Krein operator
  2264→approximation for grounding.  The schema organizes these engines under a
  2265→common typed vocabulary; it does not replace them.
  2266→\end{enumerate}
  2267→
  2268→Sections~\ref{sec:commitment}--\ref{sec:grounding} develop each regime in
  2269→this order, and Section~\ref{sec:joint} states precisely which joint and
  2270→comparative statements the typed schema does and does not support across
  2271→regimes.

  2274→\subsection{Monotonicity and Profinite Convergence}
  2275→\label{subsec:meta-monotone}
  2276→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2277→
  2278→\begin{metatheorem}[Monotonicity and Profinite Convergence]
  2279→\label{meta:monotone}
  2280→Let $\mathbb T$ be a task theory.
  2281→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  2282→\item $\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)$ is non-increasing in $M$.
  2283→\item If $\sim_\delta$ has finite index, then, $\mathcal E$ being reflexive by
  2284→Definition~\ref{def:task-theory},
  2285→\[
  2286→\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=0
  2287→\quad
  2288→\forall M\ge\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta).
  2289→\]
  2290→\item If $\mathcal E$ is separated and $\{0,\infty\}$-valued, then:
  2291→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  2292→\item for worst-case aggregation,
  2293→\[
  2294→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)=0
  2295→\text{ for some }M
  2296→\iff
  2297→\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)<\infty;
  2298→\]
  2299→\item for $\mu$-average aggregation,
  2300→\[
  2301→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0
  2302→\text{ for some }M
  2303→\iff
  2304→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)<\infty,
  2305→\]
  2306→with $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ as in
  2307→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}.  If $\mu$ has full support this
  2308→reduces to~(a).
  2309→\end{enumerate}
  2310→\item Suppose $\mathbf R$ is compact metrizable or compact uniform,
  2311→$\mathcal E$ is uniformly continuous and separated, the gap is evaluated in
  2312→the worst-case/uniform sense, and $\mathcal E$ is \emph{uniformly compatible}
  2313→with the uniformity of $\mathbf R$:
  2314→\[
  2315→\forall\,\text{entourages }U\ \exists\,\eta>0:\quad
  2316→\max\{\mathcal E(X,Z),\mathcal E(Y,Z)\}<\eta
  2317→\ \Longrightarrow\ (X,Y)\in U .
  2318→\]
  2319→Conversely, closeness in $\mathbf R$ implies small cost, by uniform continuity
  2320→of $\mathcal E$.  On a compact metric space with
  2321→$\mathcal E(X,Z)=0\iff X=Z$, joint continuity already implies this
  2322→compatibility; stating it explicitly is what makes the passage from ``small
  2323→cost to a common representative'' to ``close residual objects'' valid without
  2324→a hidden asymmetry.  Then the following are equivalent:
  2325→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  2326→\item $\delta$ is uniformly continuous for the profinite uniformity;
  2327→\item $\delta$ extends continuously to $\widehat{\mathbf H}$;
  2328→\item
  2329→\[
  2330→\lim_{M\to\infty}\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)=0.
  2331→\]
  2332→\end{enumerate}
  2333→\end{enumerate}
  2334→\end{metatheorem}
  2335→
  2336→\begin{proof}
  2337→Parts (i)--(iii) follow from the definitions.  Monotonicity follows because
  2338→the feasible set of right congruences of index at most $M$ is nested:
  2339→\[
  2340→\{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M\}
  2341→\subseteq
  2342→\{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M+1\}.
  2343→\]
  2344→If $\sim_\delta$ has finite index, quotient by $\sim_\delta$ and assign each
  2345→class its common residual $\Psi([u])=\delta(u)$, which is well defined since
  2346→$u\sim_\delta v$ implies $\delta(u)=\delta(v)$.  Every history then incurs
  2347→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=\mathcal E(\delta(u),\delta(u))=0$ by
  2348→reflexivity, so the aggregated cost vanishes for any of the three aggregation
  2349→schemes.  Reflexivity is exactly what this step consumes; without it the
  2350→quotient reproduces the trajectory but need not reproduce it at zero cost.
  2351→
  2352→For the converse in~(iii), suppose $\mathcal E$ is separated and
  2353→$\{0,\infty\}$-valued and the worst-case gap is zero at budget $M$.  Then
  2354→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$ for every $u$, and separatedness upgrades
  2355→this to $\delta(u)=\Psi([u])$.  Hence histories in a common class have equal
  2356→residuals after every continuation, so the state relation refines
  2357→$\sim_\delta$ and $\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M$.  Separatedness is
  2358→what this direction consumes, and it is assumed in~(iii) but not in~(ii).
  2359→
  2360→For (iv), assume first that $\delta$ is uniformly continuous for the profinite
  2361→uniformity.  By the universal property of completion, $\delta$ extends
  2362→continuously to a map
  2363→\[
  2364→\widehat\delta:\widehat{\mathbf H}\to\mathbf R.
  2365→\]
  2366→Since $\widehat{\mathbf H}$ is compact and $\mathbf R$ is compact uniform,
  2367→$\widehat\delta$ is uniformly continuous.  Hence for every cost tolerance
  2368→$\varepsilon>0$ there is a finite clopen partition of $\widehat{\mathbf H}$,
  2369→equivalently a finite-index right congruence $\sim$, such that
  2370→\[
  2371→u\sim v
  2372→\implies
  2373→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\delta(v))<\varepsilon
  2374→\]
  2375→in the uniform sense.  Choosing representatives for each $\sim$-class gives an
  2376→$M$-state approximation with worst-case gap less than $\varepsilon$.
  2377→
  2378→Conversely, suppose $\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)\to0$ in the worst-case sense.  For
  2379→every $\varepsilon>0$, choose a finite quotient $\sim$ and representative map
  2380→$\Psi$ such that
  2381→\[
  2382→\sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
  2383→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))
  2384→<
  2385→\eta,
  2386→\]
  2387→where $\eta>0$ is chosen small enough, using compactness and separatedness of
  2388→$\mathcal E$, to ensure that whenever two histories $u,v$ are assigned the same
  2389→representative, their residual objects are $\varepsilon$-close in the uniform
  2390→structure of $\mathbf R$.  If $u\sim v$, then $\Psi([u])=\Psi([v])$, so
  2391→$\delta(u)$ and $\delta(v)$ are $\varepsilon$-close.  Thus $\delta$ is
  2392→uniformly continuous for the profinite uniformity and therefore extends
  2393→continuously to $\widehat{\mathbf H}$.
  2394→\end{proof}
  2395→
  2396→\begin{remark}[Average-case aggregation]
  2397→\label{rem:average-profinite}
  2398→Uniform worst-case approximation implies stationary average-case
  2399→approximation.  The converse direction, from average-case gap convergence to
  2400→profinite uniform continuity, requires additional observability or positivity
  2401→assumptions on the stationary process.  The profinite equivalence is therefore
  2402→stated for the worst-case/uniform gap unless such assumptions are supplied.
  2403→\end{remark}

  2406→\subsection{Boolean Zero-Error Dichotomy}
  2407→\label{subsec:meta-boolean}
  2408→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
  2409→
  2410→\begin{definition}[Observable Support Index]
  2411→\label{def:observable-support-index}
  2412→Let $\mu$ be an average-case history law on $\mathcal I^*$ and let
  2413→$S=\operatorname{supp}\mu$.  Such a $\mu$ exists for discounted-prefix
  2414→aggregation, where
  2415→$\mu(u)=(1-\beta)\beta^{|u|}\Pr[\text{prefix }u]$ is a probability measure on
  2416→finite histories.  Stationary Ces\`aro aggregation does \emph{not} in general
  2417→supply one: Definition~\ref{def:cesaro-agg} averages a cost process along a
  2418→stationary trajectory and, when the process has infinite support, the Ces\`aro
  2419→averages need not converge to the expectation under any single probability
  2420→measure on $\mathcal I^*$.  The Ces\`aro case is treated separately in
  2421→Remark~\ref{rem:cesaro-boolean}.
  2422→The \textbf{observable support index} $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)$ is
  2423→the minimum index of a right congruence $\sim$ on $\mathcal I^*$ for which
  2424→there exists a representative map $\Psi$ with
  2425→\[
  2426→\mathcal E\bigl(\delta(u),\Psi([u])\bigr)=0
  2427→\qquad
  2428→\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }u .
  2429→\]
  2430→If $S$ is right-closed and $\mu(u)>0$ for every $u\in S$, define in addition
  2431→the \emph{support-relative Nerode relation}
  2432→\[
  2433→u\sim_{\delta,S}v
  2434→\iff
  2435→\forall w\in\mathcal I^*\text{ with }uw,vw\in S:\quad
  2436→\mathbf R\bigl(\delta(uw),\delta(vw)\bigr)
  2437→=
  2438→\mathbf R\bigl(\delta(vw),\delta(uw)\bigr)
  2439→=
  2440→0 .
  2441→\]
  2442→\end{definition}
  2443→
  2444→\begin{remark}[Restriction to the Support Is Not a Congruence]
  2445→\label{rem:support-restriction-scope}
  2446→The restriction $\sim_\delta\!\restriction_S$ is not in general a right
  2447→congruence.  If $S$ is not closed under right extension, a pair $u,v\in S$ may
  2448→have continuations $uw\notin S$ witnessing $u\not\sim_\delta v$ that the
  2449→average cost never sees; and the restricted relation need not be compatible
  2450→with the transition action.  The operative object is therefore the index of a
  2451→global congruence that is observationally exact on $S$, which is what
  2452→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index} names.  For right-closed $S$ the
  2453→two indices are related by the sandwich of
  2454→Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:boolean}(ii); they coincide precisely when the
  2455→completion of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is index-preserving, and
  2456→Remark~\ref{rem:support-extension-sharp} exhibits a right-closed support where
  2457→they differ.
  2458→\end{remark}
  2459→
  2460→\begin{lemma}[Index-Preserving Extension off the Support]
  2461→\label{lem:support-extension}
  2462→Let $S\subseteq\mathcal I^*$ be right-closed, i.e.\ $u\in S$ implies $uw\in S$
  2463→for every $w\in\mathcal I^*$.  Let $\approx$ be an equivalence relation on $S$
  2464→that is \emph{stable}: $u\approx v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$ imply $ux\approx vx$.
  2465→Then there is a right congruence $\sim$ on all of $\mathcal I^*$ whose
  2466→restriction to $S$ is $\approx$, and
  2467→\[
  2468→\operatorname{index}(\sim)
  2469→=
  2470→\operatorname{index}(\approx)+c_S ,
  2471→\]
  2472→where $c_S$ is the number of classes the construction requires off $S$.  One
  2473→has $c_S=0$ when $S=\mathcal I^*$, and $c_S\le1$ whenever no history outside
  2474→$S$ has an extension inside $S$.  In general $c_S$ may be infinite, in which
  2475→case the construction yields a right congruence of infinite index and the
  2476→upper bound is vacuous; the sandwich is informative exactly when the
  2477→completion has finite index.
  2478→\end{lemma}
  2479→
  2480→\begin{proof}
  2481→Right-closure gives $u\in S\Rightarrow ux\in S$, so $S$ is forward invariant.
  2482→The complement is \emph{not} forward invariant: a history outside $S$ may have
  2483→an extension inside $S$.  For $S=\{u:u\text{ contains at least one }\mathtt b\}$,
  2484→which is right-closed, one has $\mathtt{aaa}\notin S$ and $\mathtt{aaab}\in S$.
  2485→The set $\{u:u\text{ ends in }\mathtt b\}$ would \emph{not} serve here, since
  2486→appending $\mathtt a$ leaves it and it is therefore not right-closed.  The
  2487→classes off $S$ therefore cannot be merged freely, and the construction below
  2488→is stated explicitly.
  2489→
  2490→Define $\sim$ on $\mathcal I^*$ by declaring $u\sim v$ when either
  2491→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  2492→\item $u,v\in S$ and $u\approx v$; or
  2493→\item $u,v\notin S$ and for every $y\in\mathcal I^*$ one has
  2494→$uy\in S\iff vy\in S$, and $uy\in S$ implies $uy\approx vy$.
  2495→\end{enumerate}
  2496→This is an equivalence relation, and no class meets both $S$ and its
  2497→complement.
  2498→
  2499→\emph{Right congruence.}  Let $u\sim v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$.  If $u,v\in S$
  2500→then $ux,vx\in S$ by right-closure and $ux\approx vx$ by stability, so
  2501→$ux\sim vx$.  Suppose $u,v\notin S$.  For any $y\in\mathcal I^*$, applying~(b)
  2502→to the continuation $xy$ gives $uxy\in S\iff vxy\in S$ and, when these hold,
  2503→$uxy\approx vxy$.  If $ux\notin S$ then $vx\notin S$ by~(b) with $y=x$, and the
  2504→displayed conditions are exactly~(b) for the pair $(ux,vx)$, so $ux\sim vx$.
  2505→If $ux\in S$ then~(b) with $y=x$ gives $vx\in S$ and $ux\approx vx$, so
  2506→$ux\sim vx$ by~(a).
  2507→
  2508→\emph{Index.}  The classes inside $S$ are exactly the $\approx$-classes, and
  2509→those outside are the classes of~(b); writing $c_S$ for their number gives the
  2510→stated count.  If no history outside $S$ has an extension inside $S$, then
  2511→condition~(b) is vacuous and all such histories form a single class, so
  2512→$c_S\le1$.
  2513→\end{proof}

  2515→\begin{remark}[The Extension Can Strictly Increase the Index]
  2516→\label{rem:support-extension-sharp}
  2517→The term $c_S$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is not an artifact of the
  2518→construction: it can be strictly positive even for right-closed $S$, so the
  2519→equality $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})$
  2520→is false in general.
  2521→
  2522→Let $\mathcal I=\{\mathtt a,\mathtt b\}$ and $S=\mathcal I^{+}$, the set of
  2523→nonempty words, which is right-closed.  Let the residual take two values
  2524→$A\ne B$ and set
  2525→\[
  2526→\delta(u)=
  2527→\begin{cases}
  2528→A, & u\text{ begins with }\mathtt a,\\
  2529→B, & u\text{ begins with }\mathtt b,
  2530→\end{cases}
  2531→\qquad u\in S,
  2532→\]
  2533→with $\delta(\varepsilon)$ unconstrained since $\varepsilon\notin S$.  For
  2534→$u,v\in S$ every continuation $uw,vw$ again lies in $S$ and has the residual
  2535→determined by the first letter, so $u\sim_{\delta,S}v$ exactly when $u$ and $v$
  2536→begin with the same letter:
  2537→\[
  2538→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})=2 .
  2539→\]
  2540→
  2541→Now let $\sim$ be any right congruence on $\mathcal I^*$ admitting a
  2542→representative map exact on $S$.  The class of $\varepsilon$ must send
  2543→$\mathtt a$ into the $A$-class and $\mathtt b$ into the $B$-class.  But the
  2544→$A$-class sends \emph{both} letters into the $A$-class, and the $B$-class sends
  2545→both into the $B$-class, since $u\mathtt a$ and $u\mathtt b$ begin with the same
  2546→letter as $u$.  So $\varepsilon$ has a transition pattern matching neither
  2547→class and requires a third.  Hence
  2548→\[
  2549→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=3=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+1 ,
  2550→\]
  2551→attaining $c_S=1$.  A three-state witness is $q_\varepsilon$ with
  2552→$\tau(q_\varepsilon,\mathtt a)=q_A$, $\tau(q_\varepsilon,\mathtt b)=q_B$ and
  2553→$q_A,q_B$ absorbing.
  2554→
  2555→Two consequences follow.  First, the sandwich of
  2556→Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:boolean}(ii) is sharp at both ends.  Second, the
  2557→obstruction requires $\mu(\varepsilon)=0$: if $\mu$ is a discounted-prefix law
  2558→with $\mu(\varepsilon)>0$, then $\varepsilon\in S$, and right-closure forces
  2559→$S=\mathcal I^*$, whence $c_S=0$ and the equality does hold.
  2560→\end{remark}

  2562→\begin{metatheorem}[Boolean Zero-Error Dichotomy]
  2563→\label{meta:boolean}
  2564→Let $\mathbb T$ be separated with $\mathcal E$ $\{0,\infty\}$-valued.
  2565→
  2566→\emph{(i) Worst-case aggregation.}  If
  2567→\[
  2568→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)
  2569→=
  2570→\inf_{\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M}
  2571→\ \sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
  2572→\mathcal E\bigl(\delta(u),\Psi([u])\bigr),
  2573→\]
  2574→then
  2575→\[
  2576→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\sup}(M)=0
  2577→\iff
  2578→\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M.
  2579→\]
  2580→
  2581→\emph{(ii) Average-case aggregation.}  If the aggregation is discounted-prefix
  2582→with history law $\mu$ on $\mathcal I^*$, then
  2583→\[
  2584→\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0
  2585→\iff
  2586→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M,
  2587→\]
  2588→with $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observable support index of
  2589→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}.  If $\mu$ has full support on
  2590→$\mathcal I^*$ then
  2591→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)$ and~(ii)
  2592→reduces to~(i).  If $S=\operatorname{supp}\mu$ is right-closed with positive
  2593→mass, then
  2594→\[
  2595→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})
  2596→\ \le\
  2597→\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)
  2598→\ \le\
  2599→\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+c_S,
  2600→\]
  2601→where $c_S$ is the number of additional classes required off $S$ by
  2602→Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}; in particular $c_S=0$ when
  2603→$S=\mathcal I^*$, and equality
  2604→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})$ holds
  2605→whenever the extension of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} is
  2606→index-preserving.
  2607→In general the average-case index may be strictly smaller than the worst-case
  2608→index, since a machine may err on $\mu$-null histories at no cost.
  2609→\end{metatheorem}

  2611→\begin{remark}[The Ces\`aro Case]
  2612→\label{rem:cesaro-boolean}
  2613→Clause~(ii) is stated for discounted-prefix aggregation because that scheme
  2614→carries a genuine probability measure on finite histories.  Stationary
  2615→Ces\`aro aggregation does not, so the phrase ``$\mu$-almost every history'' has
  2616→no direct meaning there and the clause must be reformulated.
  2617→
  2618→Two formulations are available.  On the \emph{stationary state space}, when
  2619→the process is a finite-state stationary ergodic chain with positive-mass
  2620→state set $\Splus$, the natural statement replaces histories by states: zero
  2621→Ces\`aro cost holds at budget $M$ exactly when some right congruence of index
  2622→at most $M$ is exact on $\Splus$, which is
  2623→Theorem~\ref{thm:retention-zero} in the $\{0,\infty\}$-valued case.  On
  2624→\emph{histories}, the correct surrogate for ``$\mu$-almost every'' is
  2625→\emph{zero asymptotic frequency of error}: the Ces\`aro cost vanishes exactly
  2626→when
  2627→\[
  2628→\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\,\bigl|\{t<T:\ \mathcal E(\delta(U_t),\Psi([U_t]))>0\}\bigr|
  2629→=0
  2630→\]
  2631→almost surely.  For a finite-state ergodic chain the two agree, since a state
  2632→of positive stationary mass is visited with positive asymptotic frequency.
  2633→Outside that setting the second is the operative condition, and no formula in
  2634→terms of a support-relative Nerode relation is claimed.
  2635→\end{remark}
  2636→
  2637→\begin{proof}
  2638→\emph{(i)} If $\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le M$, quotienting by
  2639→$\sim_\delta$ and assigning each class its common residual gives an $M$-state
  2640→approximation with zero cost at every history.  Conversely, suppose an
  2641→$M$-state approximation has zero worst-case cost.  Then for \emph{every}
  2642→history $u$ we have $\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$, so by separatedness
  2643→$\delta(u)=\Psi([u])$.  Hence any two histories in the same machine state have
  2644→equal residuals, and, the state relation being a right congruence, equal
  2645→residuals after every continuation; that is, the state relation refines
  2646→$\sim_\delta$.  Therefore
  2647→$\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)\le\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M$.
  2648→
  2649→\emph{(ii)} Suppose $\Delta_{\mathbb T}^{\mu}(M)=0$.  Then there are a right
  2650→congruence $\sim$ of index at most $M$ and a map $\Psi$ with
  2651→$\mathcal E(\delta(u),\Psi([u]))=0$ for $\mu$-almost every $u$.  That is
  2652→precisely the defining property in
  2653→Definition~\ref{def:observable-support-index}, so
  2654→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M$.
  2655→
  2656→Conversely, if $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)\le M$, take a witnessing
  2657→congruence and representative map; the induced $M$-state machine has zero
  2658→$\mu$-average cost by construction.
  2659→
  2660→If $\mu$ has full support, ``$\mu$-almost every'' is ``every'', and the
  2661→argument of~(i) applies verbatim, giving
  2662→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)=\operatorname{index}(\sim_\delta)$.
  2663→
  2664→If $S$ is right-closed with positive mass, then for $u,v\in S$ the constraint
  2665→is exactly that all continuations \emph{within $S$} agree, which is
  2666→$\sim_{\delta,S}$.  Right-closure makes $\sim_{\delta,S}$ stable under the
  2667→transition action in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}: if
  2668→$u\sim_{\delta,S}v$ and $x\in\mathcal I$, then for every $w$ with
  2669→$uxw,vxw\in S$ the defining condition applied to the continuation $xw$ gives
  2670→$\delta(uxw)=\delta(vxw)$, so $ux\sim_{\delta,S}vx$.
  2671→
  2672→For the lower bound, any global right congruence $\sim$ witnessing
  2673→$\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$ must, restricted to $S$, refine $\sim_{\delta,S}$: two
  2674→$\sim$-equivalent histories in $S$ receive the same representative and hence
  2675→the same residual after every continuation remaining in $S$.  Therefore
  2676→$\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})\le\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}(\delta,\mu)$.
  2677→
  2678→For the upper bound, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension} to
  2679→$\approx\,=\,\sim_{\delta,S}$ to obtain a global right congruence restricting
  2680→to it on $S$, of index $\operatorname{index}(\sim_{\delta,S})+c_S$; assigning
  2681→each class inside $S$ its common residual gives zero cost $\mu$-almost
  2682→everywhere.  This is the stated sandwich.
  2683→
  2684→The extension step is not vacuous: a history outside $S$ may have an extension
  2685→inside $S$, so the classes off $S$ cannot be merged arbitrarily without
  2686→destroying the right-congruence property.  Lemma~\ref{lem:support-extension}
  2687→supplies the correct construction, and $c_S=0$ exactly when it is
  2688→index-preserving.
  2689→\end{proof}
