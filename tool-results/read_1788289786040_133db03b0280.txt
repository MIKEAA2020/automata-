 14101→Two distinct complexities are in play and must not be conflated.  Alongside the
 14102→residual-identification quantity above, define the
 14103→\emph{state-identification complexity}
 14104→\[
 14105→\EsyncSI(\mathcal C_M)
 14106→=
 14107→\inf_{\mathcal A}\
 14108→\sup_{A\in\mathcal C_M,\ q\in Q_A}\
 14109→\mistk^{\mathrm{SI}}(\mathcal A,A,q),
 14110→\]
 14111→where $\mistk^{\mathrm{SI}}$ counts mistakes before objective~(SI) is attained,
 14112→the tables being given.  Since attaining~(RI) on an unknown machine requires
 14113→resolving the tables as well as the state,
 14114→\[
 14115→\EsyncSI(\mathcal C_M)\ \le\ \Esync(\mathcal C_M),
 14116→\]
 14117→and the two may differ by the cost of model search.  The additive statements
 14118→below decompose $\Esync$ into a state-identification part and a continuation
 14119→part, so it is $\EsyncSI$ that pairs additively with the continuation cost.
 14120→The distinction matters because on
 14121→the full class the complexity is pinned down outright
 14122→(Corollary~\ref{cor:active-theta}), so the additive form
 14123→$M\log M+\Esync(M)$ collapses to $\Theta(\Esync(M))$ there; it is on
 14124→subclasses, where the counting bound $\log_2|\mathcal C_M|$ need not be
 14125→$O(M\log M)$, that the two terms can genuinely separate.  Statements below
 14126→carrying a direct-sum or operational-equivalence hypothesis are to be read with
 14127→$\Esync$ evaluated on the relevant subclass.
 14128→\end{definition}
 14129→
 14130→\begin{proposition}[State Identification Costs Only Logarithmically Many Mistakes]
 14131→\label{prop:esyncsi-log}
 14132→Let the transition and output tables of a \emph{minimal} $M$-state machine be
 14133→given and only the initial state unknown, as in objective~(SI).  Then
 14134→\[
 14135→\EsyncSI(M)\ \le\ \bigl\lfloor\log_2M\bigr\rfloor ,
 14136→\]
 14137→for \emph{every} output alphabet with $|\mathcal O|\ge2$.  In particular the
 14138→bound carries no dependence on $|\mathcal O|$.
 14139→\end{proposition}
 14140→
 14141→\begin{proof}
 14142→Maintain the version space $V\subseteq Q$ of states consistent with the
 14143→transcript, initially $V=Q$, so $|V|\le M$.  While $|V|>1$, minimality
 14144→guarantees that some input word separates two survivors; let $x$ be the first
 14145→letter of such a word on which the survivors do not all agree in output, which
 14146→exists after advancing $V$ along finitely many letters with agreed output.
 14147→Predict the output symbol receiving a plurality of votes among $\{\lambda(s,x):
 14148→s\in V\}$ and play $x$.
 14149→
 14150→Partition $V$ by the value of $\lambda(\cdot,x)$ into output classes of sizes
 14151→$c_1\ge c_2\ge\dots\ge c_r$, where $r\le|\mathcal O|$ and $\sum_ic_i=|V|$, and
 14152→let the learner predict the plurality symbol, the one realizing $c_1$.
 14153→
 14154→If the prediction is wrong, the observed symbol realizes some class $c_j$ with
 14155→$j\ge2$, and the survivors are exactly that one class, of size
 14156→$c_j\le c_2$.  The key point is that the survivors form a \emph{single} class
 14157→rather than the union of all non-predicted classes.  Since $c_1\ge c_2$ and
 14158→$c_1+c_2\le|V|$,
 14159→\[
 14160→2c_2\ \le\ c_1+c_2\ \le\ |V|,
 14161→\qquad\text{so}\qquad
 14162→c_2\ \le\ \tfrac12|V| .
 14163→\]
 14164→Every mistake therefore at least halves the version space, whatever the size of
 14165→the output alphabet.  If the prediction is right, $V$ can only shrink.
 14166→Advancing every survivor along $x$ preserves $|V|$ or decreases it, since
 14167→$\tau(\cdot,x)$ is a function.
 14168→
 14169→Starting from $|V|\le M$ and halving at each mistake, with $|V|\ge1$
 14170→throughout, the number of mistakes is at most $\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$.  When
 14171→$|V|=1$ the current state is determined, which is objective~(SI); since
 14172→$\EsyncSI$ is an infimum over learners, the bound follows.
 14173→\end{proof}
 14174→
 14175→\begin{remark}[Why the Alphabet Drops Out]
 14176→\label{rem:halving-alphabet-free}
 14177→A coarser count replaces ``the survivors are one class of size $c_2$'' by
 14178→``the survivors are everything not predicted'', giving the weaker factor
 14179→$1-1/|\mathcal O|$ and the bound
 14180→$\log_{|\mathcal O|/(|\mathcal O|-1)}M$, which degrades as the alphabet grows:
 14181→at $M=1024$ it reads $10.0$, $17.1$, $24.1$ and $51.9$ for
 14182→$|\mathcal O|=2,3,4,8$.  That is an artefact of the counting, not of the
 14183→problem.  Because a deterministic machine sends each surviving state to exactly
 14184→one output symbol, an erring learner is left with a single class, and the
 14185→plurality rule forces that class to be no larger than half the version space.
 14186→
 14187→The same counting applies wherever the version space consists of candidates
 14188→each of which predicts a single deterministic symbol.  In particular it applies
 14189→to the machine--state pairs of Theorem~\ref{thm:active-halving}, since a
 14190→deterministic machine paired with a state emits one symbol under a given input;
 14191→the alphabet-dependent factor is unnecessary there as well.
 14192→Enumerating all integer class profiles with $|V|\le25$ and $r\le5$ confirms
 14193→$c_2\le|V|/2$ without exception, with equality exactly when $c_1=c_2=|V|/2$ and
 14194→$r=2$; larger alphabets only fragment the complement further and cannot help
 14195→the adversary.
 14196→\end{remark}
 14197→
 14198→\begin{theorem}[State Identification Costs Logarithmically Many Mistakes, Exactly]
 14199→\label{thm:esyncsi-theta}
 14200→For every output alphabet with $|\mathcal O|\ge2$ and every input alphabet
 14201→with $|\mathcal I|\ge1$,
 14202→\[
 14203→\EsyncSI(M)\ =\ \bigl\lfloor\log_2M\bigr\rfloor ,
 14204→\]
 14205→with \emph{no} dependence on $|\mathcal O|$ or $|\mathcal I|$ beyond the
 14206→requirement $|\mathcal O|\ge2$.  In particular $\EsyncSI(2^L)=L$.
 14207→
 14208→The lower bound is witnessed by a minimal $M$-state machine on which every
 14209→deterministic learner is forced to make $\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$ mistakes before
 14210→identifying the current state; every randomized learner makes at least
 14211→$\tfrac12\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$ mistakes in expectation against a fixed initial
 14212→state.
 14213→\end{theorem}
 14214→
 14215→\begin{proof}
 14216→The upper bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:esyncsi-log}, which gives
 14217→$\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$ for every $|\mathcal O|\ge2$.  For the lower bound fix
 14218→$L\ge1$, put $M=2^L$, and let
 14219→\[
 14220→Q=\{0,1\}^L,
 14221→\qquad
 14222→\mathcal O=\{0,1\}.
 14223→\]
 14224→Use a single input letter $\mathtt d$ acting as the left cyclic shift,
 14225→\[
 14226→\tau(v_1v_2\cdots v_L,\mathtt d)=v_2\cdots v_Lv_1,
 14227→\qquad
 14228→\lambda(v_1v_2\cdots v_L,\mathtt d)=v_1 ,
 14229→\]
 14230→adjoining a second letter acting as the identity with constant output if
 14231→$|\mathcal I|\ge2$ is required; that letter reveals nothing and can be ignored.
 14232→The tables are known to the learner and only the initial state is unknown, as
 14233→objective~(SI) requires.  The construction uses only two of the available
 14234→output symbols, so it is available verbatim for every $|\mathcal O|\ge2$; a
 14235→larger alphabet neither helps the learner nor the adversary here.
 14236→
 14237→\emph{Minimality.}  Two distinct states $v\ne w$ differ in some coordinate $j$,
 14238→and the word $\mathtt d^{\,j-1}$ drives them to states whose first coordinates
 14239→are $v_j\ne w_j$, so the next output separates them.  Hence all $M$ states are
 14240→pairwise distinguishable and the machine is minimal.
 14241→
 14242→\emph{The transcript is exactly the initial state.}  Applying $\mathtt d$
 14243→repeatedly from $v$ emits $v_1,v_2,\dots,v_L$ in order and returns to $v$.
 14244→Thus after $L$ rounds the state is determined, and before round $t\le L$ the
 14245→transcript constrains only $v_1,\dots,v_{t-1}$: the remaining $2^{L-t+1}$
 14246→states consistent with the transcript are exactly those with the observed
 14247→prefix, and every completion is a legal initial state.
 14248→
 14249→\emph{Deterministic lower bound.}  Run the adversary argument on the version
 14250→space.  At round $t\le L$ the learner must commit to a prediction
 14251→$\widehat y_t\in\{0,1\}$ for the bit $v_t$.  Both completions $v_t=0$ and
 14252→$v_t=1$ remain consistent, so the adversary sets $v_t=1-\widehat y_t$, forcing
 14253→a mistake, and records the bit.  After $L$ rounds the adversary has specified
 14254→an initial state $v^\star\in Q$ consistent with every revealed output, and the
 14255→learner has made $L=\log_2M$ mistakes.  Until round $L$ the version space has
 14256→size at least $2$, so the learner has not attained~(SI); the mistakes are
 14257→therefore all incurred before identification.
 14258→
 14259→\emph{Randomized lower bound.}  Let the initial state be uniform on $Q$.
 14260→Conditioned on the transcript through round $t-1$, the bit $v_t$ is a fair coin
 14261→independent of the learner's internal randomness, so any prediction errs with
 14262→probability exactly $\tfrac12$ and the expected number of mistakes over the
 14263→first $L$ rounds is $L/2$.  Averaging over the prior, some fixed
 14264→$v^\star\in Q$ forces at least $\tfrac12\log_2M$ expected mistakes against the
 14265→learner's own randomness.
 14266→
 14267→For general $M$ no padding is required, because $\mathcal H_M$ consists of the
 14268→machines with \emph{at most} $M$ states.  Put $L=\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$, so
 14269→$2^L\le M$.  The cyclic-shift machine above has exactly $2^L$ states and is
 14270→minimal, hence belongs to $\mathcal H_M$, and against it every deterministic
 14271→learner is forced into $L$ mistakes.  Since $\EsyncSI(M)$ is a supremum over
 14272→$\mathcal H_M$, this gives $\EsyncSI(M)\ge L=\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$, matching
 14273→the upper bound.
 14274→\end{proof}
 14275→
 14276→\begin{remark}[Why No Padding Is Needed]
 14277→\label{rem:esyncsi-intermediate}
 14278→Equality at intermediate budgets is a consequence of the ``at most $M$
 14279→states'' convention for $\mathcal H_M$, not of an interpolating construction.
 14280→The witness is built at $2^L$ states, and for any $M$ with $2^L\le M$ that same
 14281→machine is already a member of $\mathcal H_M$; no padding to exactly $M$ states
 14282→is required, and none is available, since adjoining duplicate states destroys
 14283→minimality while adjoining fresh reachable ones destroys the fair-bit property.
 14284→
 14285→Were $\mathcal H_M$ instead read as machines with \emph{exactly} $M$ states,
 14286→the argument would give only $\EsyncSI(M)=\Theta(\log M)$, with the constant
 14287→lost to monotonicity.  The two readings agree on the powers of two.
 14288→\end{remark}
 14289→
 14290→\begin{remark}[Both Bounds Are Attained by the Same Mechanism]
 14291→\label{rem:esyncsi-tight}
 14292→The matching bounds have a common explanation.  The halving argument of
 14293→Proposition~\ref{prop:esyncsi-log} shows that each mistake removes at least
 14294→half of the version space, so $\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$ mistakes suffice; the
 14295→cyclic-shift family of Theorem~\ref{thm:esyncsi-theta} is the case in which
 14296→each mistake removes exactly half and no more, because each round exposes one
 14297→fresh, unconstrained bit of the initial state.  The family is thus extremal for
 14298→the halving bound, and the two sides meet exactly rather than up to a
 14299→constant.
 14300→
 14301→Solving the associated games exactly confirms both sides.  For
 14302→$L=1,\dots,10$ the deterministic minimax mistake count on the cyclic-shift
 14303→family is exactly $L=\log_2M$ and the Bayes-optimal expected count under the
 14304→uniform prior is exactly $L/2$, with all $M$ states pairwise separated so that
 14305→the family is minimal.  In the opposite direction, exhausting all minimal Mealy
 14306→machines across nine signatures with $|\Splus|\le4$, $|\mathcal I|\le2$ and
 14307→$|\mathcal O|\le4$ --- $46{,}656$ table pairs in the largest, of which
 14308→$35{,}640$ are minimal --- the worst-case state-identification cost never
 14309→exceeds $\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$, and attains it at $M=2$ and $M=4$.  Holding
 14310→$|\Splus|$ fixed and enlarging $|\mathcal O|$ from $2$ to $4$ leaves the worst
 14311→case unchanged, as Remark~\ref{rem:halving-alphabet-free} predicts.
 14312→
 14313→Two consequences follow for the structure of the theory.  First, the collapse
 14314→recorded in Remark~\ref{rem:additive-collapses} is now unconditional: it rests
 14315→on a two-sided estimate rather than on an upper bound alone, so no choice of
 14316→subclass can rescue a mistake-currency additive decomposition.  Second, there
 14317→is no dependence on $|\mathcal O|$ at all.  Both bounds equal
 14318→$\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor$: the upper bound because an erring learner is left with
 14319→a single output class of size at most half the version space
 14320→(Remark~\ref{rem:halving-alphabet-free}), the lower bound because the
 14321→cyclic-shift witness needs only two output symbols.  Enlarging the alphabet
 14322→neither speeds up identification nor slows it down, and the base of the
 14323→logarithm is $2$ irrespective of $|\mathcal O|$.
 14324→\end{remark}
 14325→
 14326→\begin{remark}[The Additive Form Collapses]
 14327→\label{rem:additive-collapses}
 14328→Proposition~\ref{prop:esyncsi-log}, sharpened to an equality by
 14329→Theorem~\ref{thm:esyncsi-theta}, has a structural consequence for the
 14330→additive decomposition.  Since $\EsyncSI(\mathcal C_M)=O(\log M)$ for every
 14331→subclass of minimal machines,
 14332→\[
 14333→M\log M+\EsyncSI(\mathcal C_M)=\Theta(M\log M),
 14334→\]
 14335→so the two terms never separate and the additive form carries no information
 14336→beyond $\Theta(M\log M)$.  Accordingly no additive theorem is asserted with a
 14337→state-identification second term.
 14338→
 14339→A genuine two-term decomposition would require measuring the synchronization
 14340→component in a currency other than mistakes --- experiment \emph{length}, as in
 14341→$\Lsyncu$ of Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}, is the natural candidate,
 14342→and Proposition~\ref{prop:active-length-upper} is stated in that currency.  The
 14343→mistake-counting decomposition is degenerate.
 14344→\end{remark}

 14346→\begin{lemma}[Operational Equivalence]
 14347→\label{lem:operational-equivalence}
 14348→Let the class be a set of deterministic machines and let the protocol be
 14349→realizable.  Then a learner guarantees zero future mistakes on all
 14350→continuations from a given history if and only if it has attained
 14351→objective~(RI) of Definition~\ref{def:residual-knowledge} at that history.
 14352→
 14353→For deterministic realizable classes this is therefore a theorem, not a
 14354→hypothesis; it is stated separately because the results below that invoke it
 14355→are also meaningful for stochastic classes, where it must be assumed.
 14356→\end{lemma}
 14357→
 14358→\begin{proof}
 14359→\emph{(RI) implies zero future mistakes.}  This is the prediction-closing
 14360→property recorded in Definition~\ref{def:residual-knowledge}: the continuation
 14361→function from the current history is determined by the transcript, uniformly
 14362→over consistent targets, and realizability places the true target among them,
 14363→so predicting by that common function is correct on every subsequent round.
 14364→
 14365→\emph{Zero future mistakes implies (RI).}  Suppose~(RI) has not been attained,
 14366→so two machines $A,A'$ in the class are consistent with the transcript and
 14367→their continuation functions from the current histories differ.  Let $w$ be a
 14368→shortest word on which the two continuation functions disagree, which exists by
 14369→Lemma~\ref{lem:moore-separation}, and let $j$ be the first position at which
 14370→the emitted symbols differ.  The learner's prediction at that round is a
 14371→function of the transcript alone, hence identical in the run against $A$ and in
 14372→the run against $A'$, while the two true symbols differ.  Both runs are
 14373→realizable, since $A$ and $A'$ both lie in the class and both are consistent
 14374→with everything observed.  The learner therefore errs in at least one of them,
 14375→contradicting the guarantee of zero future mistakes on all continuations.
 14376→\end{proof}

 14378→\begin{definition}[Direct-Sum Active Instance]
 14379→\label{def:direct-sum-active}
 14380→A \textbf{direct-sum active instance} of budget $M$ consists of an
 14381→$O(M)$-state machine family together with a decomposition of the learning task
 14382→into two components satisfying:
 14383→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 14384→\item a synchronization component whose minimax mistake complexity is $S_M$,
 14385→measuring \emph{state identification only} and so comparable to
 14386→$\EsyncSI$ rather than to $\Esync$;
 14387→\item a continuation component whose active realizable mistake complexity is
 14388→$C_M$, measuring the residual model search that remains once the state is
 14389→known;
 14390→\item \textbf{disjoint accounting}: the rounds on which the two components can
 14391→force mistakes are disjoint;
 14392→\item \textbf{unavoidability}: the objective cannot be met without meeting it
 14393→for \emph{both} components, and this is a property of the machine family
 14394→rather than an external restriction on the protocol;
 14395→\item \textbf{product structure}: the family is parameterized by a pair
 14396→$(\theta_1,\theta_2)$ of unknowns, one per component, and \emph{every}
 14397→combination occurs, so the family is the full product of its two coordinate
 14398→ranges;
 14399→\item \textbf{coordinate locality}: the outputs on the rounds of $R_j$ are
 14400→determined by $\theta_j$ alone, independently of $\theta_{3-j}$.
 14401→\end{enumerate}
 14402→Conditions~(iii)--(vi) together constitute the \emph{component-restriction
 14403→property}.  It is exactly what the reduction in
 14404→Theorem~\ref{thm:active-direct-sum} consumes: (vi) makes a component's
 14405→observations well defined once its own parameter is fixed, (v) guarantees that
 14406→an arbitrary dummy value of the other parameter yields a legitimate composite
 14407→target, (iii) keeps the two mistake counts from overlapping, and (iv) forces
 14408→both components to be paid for.
 14409→Condition~(iv) is essential in the active protocol, where the learner chooses
 14410→its own inputs.  A family in which one component can be bypassed -- for
 14411→example by an irreversible transition into the other component -- does not
 14412→satisfy~(iv), and for such a family the additive bound fails.
 14413→Theorem~\ref{thm:active-two-phase} constructs a family satisfying~(iv) by
 14414→making the two components mutually reachable.
 14415→\end{definition}

 14417→\begin{theorem}[Active Realizable Certified Bounds]
 14418→\label{thm:active-certified}
 14419→For active realizable learning over $M$-state deterministic machines, the
 14420→following three statements hold under \emph{separate} hypotheses, which are
 14421→not to be combined implicitly.
 14422→
 14423→\medskip
 14424→\noindent\textbf{(I) Upper bound (unconditional).}
 14425→Under objective~(RI) of Definition~\ref{def:residual-knowledge},
 14426→\[
 14427→\MistRI(M)
 14428→\ \le\
 14429→O\bigl(\Esync(M)\bigr),
 14430→\]
 14431→and this is sharp: by Definition~\ref{def:sync-mistake-complexity} some
 14432→learner attains~(RI) within $\Esync(M)$ mistakes, and~(RI) is
 14433→prediction-closing, so no mistake occurs afterwards.  No continuation term is
 14434→appended.
 14435→
 14436→\noindent\textbf{(II) Synchronization lower bound (needs
 14437→Lemma~\ref{lem:operational-equivalence} alone).}
 14438→If any learner guaranteeing zero future mistakes must first acquire
 14439→guaranteed residual-class knowledge, then
 14440→\[
 14441→\MistRI(M)
 14442→\ge
 14443→\Omega\bigl(\Esync(M)\bigr).
 14444→\]
 14445→This gives \emph{only} the $\Esync$ term; it does not by itself supply the
 14446→$M\log M$ term.
 14447→
 14448→\noindent\textbf{(III) Disjoint-component lower bound (needs direct-sum
 14449→saturation, Definition~\ref{def:direct-sum-active}).}
 14450→On a subclass $\mathcal C_M$ carrying a direct-sum instance with component
 14451→complexities $S_M$ and $C_M$,
 14452→\[
 14453→\MistRI(M)\ \ge\ S_M+C_M .
 14454→\]
 14455→This is a lower bound only; no matching upper bound is asserted, and no
 14456→identification of $S_M$ with a state-identification complexity is made.  By
 14457→Proposition~\ref{prop:esyncsi-log} the state-identification complexity is
 14458→$O(\log M)$, so a decomposition with $\EsyncSI$ as its second term would be
 14459→degenerate (Remark~\ref{rem:additive-collapses}); the components realized by
 14460→the explicit construction of Theorem~\ref{thm:active-two-phase} are both
 14461→model-identification components.
 14462→
 14463→\medskip
 14464→Parts~(II) and~(III) are stated in terms of the abstract complexity $\Esync$
 14465→and are the form that remains informative on \emph{subclasses}
 14466→$\mathcal C_M\subseteq\mathcal H_M$, where the two terms of the additive form
 14467→can separate.  On the full class $\mathcal H_M$ no hypothesis is needed at all:
 14468→Corollary~\ref{cor:active-theta} gives
 14469→$\MistRI(M)=\Theta(M\log M)$ outright, and
 14470→$M\log M+\Esync(M)=\Theta(\Esync(M))$ there.  An explicit family satisfying the
 14471→hypothesis of~(III) is constructed in
 14472→Theorem~\ref{thm:active-two-phase}, so~(III) is nonvacuous.
 14473→\end{theorem}
 14474→
 14475→\begin{proof}
 14476→\emph{Part~(I).}  Fix $\varepsilon>0$.  By
 14477→Definition~\ref{def:sync-mistake-complexity} the infimum over learners is
 14478→attained to within $\varepsilon$, so there is an active learner
 14479→$\mathcal A_\varepsilon$ which, on every $A\in\mathcal H_M$ and every initial
 14480→state, attains objective~(RI) after at most $\Esync(M)+\varepsilon$ mistakes.
 14481→Run $\mathcal A_\varepsilon$.  At the moment of attainment the continuation
 14482→function from the current history is determined by the transcript, uniformly
 14483→over all targets still consistent with it; since the protocol is realizable,
 14484→the true target is among them, and predicting according to that common
 14485→continuation function is correct on every subsequent round.  Hence the total
 14486→number of mistakes is at most $\Esync(M)+\varepsilon$.  As $\varepsilon>0$ was
 14487→arbitrary and the mistake count is integer valued, the bound
 14488→$O(\Esync(M))$ follows.
 14489→
 14490→No continuation term appears, since objective~(RI) already fixes the
 14491→continuation function from the current history.
 14492→
 14493→\emph{Part~(II).}  By Lemma~\ref{lem:operational-equivalence}, any
 14494→learner that eventually guarantees correct prediction on all continuations must
 14495→first attain objective~(RI).  By
 14496→Definition~\ref{def:sync-mistake-complexity} the infimum over learners of the
 14497→worst-case number of mistakes before that point is $\Esync(M)$, so every
 14498→learner incurs at least $\Esync(M)$ mistakes in the worst case:
 14499→\[
 14500→\MistRI(M)\ \ge\ \Omega(\Esync(M)).
 14501→\]
 14502→This uses no direct-sum hypothesis and yields \emph{only} the $\Esync$ term.
 14503→
 14504→\emph{Part~(III).}  Assume the direct-sum saturation condition of
 14505→Definition~\ref{def:direct-sum-active}.  The Littlestone lower bound is proved
 14506→against adversarial inputs and does not transfer automatically to the active
 14507→protocol (Remark~\ref{rem:no-automatic-active-lower}), so each component's
 14508→cost must be supplied by that hypothesis rather than by counting.
 14509→
 14510→The bound is $S_M+C_M$, not $\max\{S_M,C_M\}$, and the additivity comes from
 14511→disjointness rather than from any inequality between maxima and sums.  By
 14512→condition~(iv) the objective forces both components; by condition~(iii) the
 14513→rounds on which they force mistakes are disjoint; and by
 14514→Theorem~\ref{thm:active-direct-sum}, whose restriction argument uses
 14515→conditions~(v) and~(vi), each component contributes at least its own minimax
 14516→complexity on those rounds.  Summing over the two disjoint round sets gives
 14517→\[
 14518→\MistRI(M)\ \ge\ S_M+C_M .
 14519→\]
 14520→No matching upper bound is claimed: Part~(I) bounds $\MistRI(M)$ by
 14521→$O(\Esync(\mathcal C_M))$, and identifying $\Esync(\mathcal C_M)$ with
 14522→$S_M+C_M$ would require an explicit composite learner, which is not
 14523→constructed here.
 14524→\end{proof}

 14526→\begin{proposition}[Length-Based Active Upper Bound]
 14527→\label{prop:active-length-upper}
 14528→For fixed finite alphabets --- the hypothesis $\Lsyncu(M)<\infty$ being
 14529→automatic by Proposition~\ref{prop:lsyncu-version-space} ---
 14530→\[
 14531→\MistRI(M)
 14532→\le
 14533→O\bigl(M\log M+\Lsyncu(M)\bigr),
 14534→\]
 14535→and in particular
 14536→\[
 14537→\Esync(M)\ \le\ O\bigl(M\log M+\Lsyncu(M)\bigr).
 14538→\]
 14539→The universal depth, not the machine-specific $\Lsync(M)$, is the correct
 14540→quantity here: a learner facing an unknown machine must fix its experiment in
 14541→advance, so it cannot in general realize $L_{\mathrm{sync}}^{\mathrm{adapt}}(A)$
 14542→for the particular $A$ it faces.  The two terms are not redundant: $\Lsyncu(M)$
 14543→pays for state identification and the $M\log M$ term for the subsequent model
 14544→search, these being the two things a universal experiment cannot deliver at
 14545→once (Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}).  If the machine is known except
 14546→for its initial state, the model-search phase is vacuous and the bound reads
 14547→$O(\Lsync(M))$.
 14548→
 14549→In order of magnitude this bound is subsumed by
 14550→Theorem~\ref{thm:active-halving}, which attains the same objective in
 14551→$O(M\log M)$ mistakes without any synchronization experiment; even for
 14552→$\Lsyncu(M)=o(M\log M)$ the total here remains $O(M\log M)$.  Its interest is
 14553→structural.  It separates the contribution of the synchronization experiment
 14554→from that of the model search, and its first phase is governed by an experiment
 14555→fixable in advance, which is the operative quantity when the model-search phase
 14556→is externally curtailed.
 14557→No lower bound is asserted here; see
 14558→Remark~\ref{rem:no-automatic-active-lower}.
 14559→\end{proposition}
 14560→
 14561→\begin{proof}
 14562→For the upper bound, the learner fixes in advance a universal adaptive
 14563→synchronization tree of worst-case depth $\Lsyncu(M)$, as in
 14564→Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}; this requires no knowledge of the
 14565→target machine.  During this phase it makes at most $\Lsyncu(M)$ prediction
 14566→mistakes, one per step in the worst case.  By
 14567→Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync} the observed transcript then determines
 14568→the current state of the target within each machine still consistent with it;
 14569→the tables, and hence the residual class, are not yet determined, so
 14570→objective~(RI) has not been attained and a second phase is required.
 14571→
 14572→The learner then runs the standard halving algorithm over the set of machines
 14573→in $\mathcal H_M$ consistent with the transcript so far, of cardinality at
 14574→most $|\mathcal H_M|$.  Each mistake halves that set, so at most
 14575→$\log_2|\mathcal H_M|=O(M\log M)$ further mistakes occur before a single
 14576→residual class remains, at which point objective~(RI) is attained and, being
 14577→prediction-closing, no mistake occurs afterwards.  The two phases together
 14578→give
 14579→\[
 14580→O(M\log M+\Lsyncu(M)).
 14581→\]
 14582→Here the $M\log M$ term is part of the cost of \emph{attaining}~(RI), not a
 14583→continuation term appended after attainment; this is the distinction drawn in
 14584→Remark~\ref{rem:objective-bookkeeping}.
 14585→
 14586→For the second display, the composite strategy just described is one
 14587→admissible learner attaining~(RI), and it is implementable without knowing the
 14588→target.  Since $\Esync(M)$ is an infimum over such learners
 14589→(Definition~\ref{def:sync-mistake-complexity}), the bound
 14590→$\Esync(M)\le O(M\log M+\Lsyncu(M))$ follows; when
 14591→$\Lsyncu(M)=\Omega(M\log M)$ this reads $\Esync(M)=O(\Lsyncu(M))$.
 14592→
 14593→\end{proof}

 14595→\begin{proposition}[Machine-Specific Synchronization Depth Is Quadratic]
 14596→\label{prop:lsyncu-quadratic}
 14597→For fixed finite alphabets and every $M\ge2$,
 14598→\[
 14599→\Lsync(M)\ \le\ (M-1)^2\ =\ O(M^2).
 14600→\]
 14601→The bound is on the \emph{machine-specific} depth $\Lsync$, in which the
 14602→experiment may depend on the target; see
 14603→Remark~\ref{rem:lsync-not-lsyncu} for why it does not transfer to the
 14604→universal depth $\Lsyncu$.
 14605→\end{proposition}
 14606→
 14607→\begin{proof}
 14608→Fix $A\in\mathcal H_M$, minimal without loss of generality by the convention of
 14609→Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}, and let $U\subseteq Q_A$ be the set of
 14610→states consistent with the transcript so far, initially $U=Q_A$ with
 14611→$|U|\le M$.  We exhibit a strategy, fixable in advance, that drives $U$ to a
 14612→single observational class.
 14613→
 14614→\emph{Step: one separation episode.}  Suppose $U$ contains two states $s\ne t$
 14615→that are not observationally equivalent.  Consider the pair automaton on
 14616→unordered pairs of states of $A$: from $\{u,v\}$ and input $x$ there is an edge
 14617→to $\{\tau(u,x),\tau(v,x)\}$, and $\{u,v\}$ is \emph{immediately separated} by
 14618→$x$ when $\lambda(u,x)\ne\lambda(v,x)$.  Breadth-first search from $\{s,t\}$
 14619→reaches an immediately separated pair, since $s\not\sim t$; the pair automaton
 14620→has at most $\binom{M}{2}$ nodes, but the standard argument bounds the distance
 14621→by $M-1$, because a shortest such path visits pairwise distinct pairs whose
 14622→underlying state sets are nested along the search and cannot repeat.  Let $w$
 14623→be the corresponding word, $|w|\le M-1$.
 14624→
 14625→Feed $w$.  Transitions are deterministic, so applying an input maps $U$ forward
 14626→and $|U|$ never increases.  At the final letter of $w$ the two survivors
 14627→descended from $s$ and $t$ emit different outputs, so whichever output is
 14628→observed at least one of the two branches is eliminated and $|U|$ strictly
 14629→decreases.
 14630→
 14631→\emph{Aggregation.}  Each episode costs at most $M-1$ inputs and strictly
 14632→decreases $|U|$.  Since $|U|$ starts at most $M$ and terminates when all
 14633→surviving states are observationally equivalent, at most $M-1$ episodes occur,
 14634→giving total depth at most $(M-1)^2$.
 14635→
 14636→The strategy runs breadth-first search on the pair automaton \emph{of the
 14637→target $A$}, so it presupposes knowledge of $A$ and is admissible for
 14638→$\Lsync(A)$.  Taking the supremum over $A\in\mathcal H_M$ gives the stated
 14639→bound on $\Lsync(M)$.
 14640→\end{proof}

 14642→\begin{proposition}[Universal Synchronization Depth, Version-Space Form]
 14643→\label{prop:lsyncu-version-space}
 14644→Let
 14645→\[
 14646→N_M=\bigl|\{(A,q):A\in\mathcal H_M,\ q\in Q_A\}\bigr|\ \le\ M\,|\mathcal H_M| .
 14647→\]
 14648→Then
 14649→\[
 14650→\Lsync(M)\ \le\ \Lsyncu(M)\ \le\ (M-1)(N_M-1)\ <\ \infty .
 14651→\]
 14652→In particular $\Lsyncu(M)$ is finite for fixed finite alphabets and fixed $M$,
 14653→so the hypothesis $\Lsyncu(M)<\infty$ of
 14654→Proposition~\ref{prop:active-length-upper} is automatic.
 14655→\end{proposition}
 14656→
 14657→\begin{proof}
 14658→Maintain the version space $V\subseteq\{(A,q):A\in\mathcal H_M,\ q\in Q_A\}$ of
 14659→machine--state pairs consistent with the transcript so far, initially all of
 14660→them.  The whole strategy is a function of the transcript, since $V$ is, and it
 14661→is therefore a single decision tree in the sense of
 14662→Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}; this is the point on which the
 14663→machine-specific bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:lsyncu-quadratic} fail and
 14664→this one does not.
 14665→
 14666→While some machine $A$ occurring in $V$ carries two candidates $(A,q)$ and
 14667→$(A,q')$ in $V$ whose states are not observationally equivalent in $A$, select
 14668→one such triple.  The learner may compute inside $A$ because $A$ is a
 14669→\emph{candidate}, named by the version space, not the unknown target.  Both $q$
 14670→and $q'$ are states of the same $M$-state machine, so
 14671→Lemma~\ref{lem:moore-separation} applied within $A$ --- rather than to a
 14672→disjoint union of two machines --- supplies a word $w$ with
 14673→$\lambda_A(q,w)\ne\lambda_A(q',w)$ and $|w|\le M-1$.
 14674→
 14675→Feed $w$ and delete from $V$ every candidate whose predicted output sequence
 14676→disagrees with the observation, advancing the survivors along $w$.  At least
 14677→one of $(A,q)$, $(A,q')$ is deleted: their predicted sequences differ, so at
 14678→most one can agree with whatever is observed.  Hence $|V|$ strictly decreases
 14679→in each episode.  Starting from $|V|=N_M$ and terminating when no consistent
 14680→machine carries two observationally distinct candidate states, at most
 14681→$N_M-1$ episodes occur, each of length at most $M-1$.
 14682→
 14683→At termination the transcript determines the current state of the target within
 14684→every machine still consistent with it, which is precisely the objective of
 14685→Definition~\ref{def:output-aware-sync}.
 14686→\end{proof}

 14688→\begin{remark}[Finiteness Is Now Unconditional; the Rate Is Not]
 14689→\label{rem:lsyncu-finite-not-rate}
 14690→Proposition~\ref{prop:lsyncu-version-space} settles finiteness, which
 14691→Remark~\ref{rem:lsync-not-lsyncu} had left open once $|\mathcal I|\ge2$, but it
 14692→does not settle the rate.  The bound is polynomial in the number of candidates
 14693→$N_M\le M|\mathcal H_M|$ and hence exponential in $M$, since
 14694→$|\mathcal H_M|$ is itself exponential; it is therefore far weaker in $M$ than
 14695→the machine-specific $\binom{M}{2}$.  Whether $\Lsyncu(M)$ is polynomial in $M$
 14696→is Conjecture~\ref{conj:lsyncu-poly}, and whether it is $O(M\log M)$ is the
 14697→surviving branch of Open Problem~\ref{open:si-hard-family}.
 14698→\end{remark}
