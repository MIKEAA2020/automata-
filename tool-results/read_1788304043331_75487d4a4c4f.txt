   110→\bfseries
   111→The Rate--Distortion Theory of Bounded Sequential Transduction:\\
   112→A Comparative Syntax for Finite-State Approximation
   113→}
   114→\author{}
   115→\date{}

   120→%======================================================================
   121→\begin{abstract}
   122→We develop a comparative framework for finite-state approximation of
   123→sequential objects: a rate--distortion theory of bounded sequential
   124→transduction in which the rate is a state budget $M$ and the distortion is a
   125→regime-specific task loss.  Three regimes share one syntax --- histories,
   126→finite-index right congruences, quotient machines, stationary aggregation, and
   127→variational gaps --- and differ in their semantic objects.  Commitment targets
   128→deterministic specifications through deterministic Mealy machines and
   129→Myhill--Nerode residuals.  Retention targets stochastic controlled processes
   130→through stationary controlled unifilar causal machines (controlled
   131→$\epsilon$-machines, whose state update may read the realized output) and
   132→their lumpable quotients.  Grounding targets stochastic channels through
   133→linear finite-rank Hankel realizations, whose resource is operator rank rather
   134→than quotient index; a closed-form finite-budget characterization of exact
   135→symbolic deterministic grounding remains open, but an observable deterministic
   136→floor, its finite-state limit, and certified finite-horizon and finite-section
   137→intervals are established.  Every approximation claim carries an explicit type
   138→signature fixing regime, feasible set, aggregation, support, protocol, and
   139→analytical bridge, so that statements from different regimes are not
   140→conflated.
   141→
   142→The principal exact results are as follows.  The retention full-KL gap admits
   143→an exact finite-state information-bottleneck decomposition over stationary
   144→lumpable quotients, with a controlled analogue, conditional on the input, for
   145→general unifilar machines, and a zero-retention threshold at the stationary
   146→support size of distinct predictive states in the input-driven theory and at
   147→the coarsest unifilar-lumpable refinement of the predictive-kernel partition
   148→in general.  For finite output alphabets a global spectral converse holds in
   149→probability coordinates with sharp constant $1$; under uniform interiority a
   150→corresponding bound holds in a fixed minimal natural-parameter chart, and the
   151→interiority hypothesis cannot be removed.  The unrestricted linear finite-rank
   152→Hankel relaxation gap is $\Dunres(M)=\sigma_{M+1}(H_\nu)$ by
   153→Eckart--Young--Mirsky, while the Hankel-structured gap is bounded below by
   154→$\sigma_{M+1}(H_\nu)$ and equals it under an Adamjan--Arov--Krein
   155→Hardy-space embedding --- unconditional for one-letter alphabets, conditional
   156→on a multiletter Nehari/AAK-type theorem otherwise, which is not established
   157→here.  A superpolynomial, sub-exponential determinism gap separates formal
   158→Hankel rank from deterministic state complexity.  Retention is NP-complete in
   159→the Gaussian quadratic restricted regime and NP-hard under a promise with
   160→APX-hardness for unrestricted full KL.  Mistake complexity is $\Theta(M\log
   161→M)$ in the reset-word, persistent-stream, and active residual-identification
   162→protocols, with agnostic regret $\Theta(\sqrt{TM\log M})$.
   163→
   164→The framework separates shared syntax from regime-specific analysis: a typed
   165→variational schema; structural monotonicity and zero thresholds; a conditional
   166→response-operator converse and Schatten template; adaptive oracle
   167→inequalities with a matching minimax lower bound under explicit floors; and a
   168→linear second-order Price-of-Safety surrogate with a Ky Fan majorization law.
   169→A vertex correspondence organizes retention, commitment, and grounding by
   170→R\'enyi/Schatten labels $\{0,1,\infty\}$ as a formal consistency rather
   171→than a derivation, and no analytic theorem depends on it.  An independence
   172→theorem shows that the three regimes' obstructions vary independently, so no
   173→cross-regime ordering holds.  The average-case theory uses stationary
   174→Ces\`aro or discounted prefix aggregation throughout, finite prefixes not
   175→defining a probability measure over all lengths.  The manuscript identifies a
   176→small set of sharply posed open research programs in place of a single
   177→undifferentiated open problem of exact symbolic deterministic grounding.
   178→\end{abstract}

   181→%======================================================================
   182→\section{Introduction: The Bounded Sequential Generator and Its Regimes}
   183→\label{sec:intro}
   184→%======================================================================
   185→
   186→This manuscript develops a comparative theory of finite-state approximation
   187→for sequential objects.  The resource is memory, measured by the number of
   188→internal states or, equivalently, by the index of a finite right congruence on
   189→histories.  The cost is a regime-specific distortion functional: Boolean
   190→mismatch for commitment, KL divergence for retention, operator-norm distance
   191→for linear grounding relaxations, and related quantitative costs.
   192→
   193→The framework may be viewed as a rate--distortion theory of bounded sequential
   194→transduction.  The rate is the finite-state budget $M$.  The distortion is the
   195→task loss induced by the regime.
   196→
   197→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   198→\subsection{Bounded sequential generators}
   199→\label{subsec:bsg-intro}
   200→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   201→
   202→A \textbf{Bounded Sequential Generator} (BSG) is a deterministic finite-state
   203→transducer, i.e.\ a Mealy machine,
   204→\[
   205→\mathcal A=(\mathcal S,s_0,\delta,\lambda),
   206→\qquad
   207→|\mathcal S|\le M,
   208→\]
   209→with finite input alphabet $\mathcal I$, finite output alphabet $\mathcal O$,
   210→transition map
   211→\[
   212→\delta:\mathcal S\times\mathcal I\to\mathcal S,
   213→\]
   214→and output map
   215→\[
   216→\lambda:\mathcal S\times\mathcal I\to\mathcal O.
   217→\]
   218→It defines a synchronous causal function
   219→\[
   220→F_{\mathcal A}:\mathcal I^{\mathbb N}\to\mathcal O^{\mathbb N}.
   221→\]
   222→
   223→The deterministic Mealy BSG is the machine model for the commitment regime.
   224→In that regime, the target is a deterministic specification, a safety
   225→property, or a deterministic strategic rule, and the question is whether a
   226→finite-state machine implements or enforces it exactly or approximately.  The
   227→exact zero-error theory is governed by Myhill--Nerode residual equivalence.
   228→
   229→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   230→\subsection{Three approximation regimes}
   231→\label{subsec:three-regimes}
   232→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   233→
   234→The manuscript studies three regimes.  They share a finite-state syntax but
   235→differ in their semantic objects.  The framework does not reduce the three
   236→regimes to a single machine category.
   237→
   238→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em]
   239→\item \textbf{Commitment.}
   240→The target is a deterministic residual function.  A history $u$ determines a
   241→residual continuation map
   242→\[
   243→F_u(w)=F(uw)_{|u|+1:|u|+|w|}.
   244→\]
   245→Two histories are equivalent if they induce the same residual function.  The
   246→minimal exact state complexity is the Myhill--Nerode index
   247→\[
   248→\kappa_{\det}(F)
   249→=
   250→\operatorname{index}(\sim_F).
   251→\]
   252→The exact commitment gap is a threshold phenomenon:
   253→\[
   254→\Com(M)=0
   255→\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   256→M\ge\kappa_{\det}(F).
   257→\]
   258→
   259→\item \textbf{Retention.}
   260→The target is a stochastic controlled process whose predictive structure is
   261→encoded by causal states.  Each positive-mass causal state $s\in\Splus$
   262→carries a predictive distribution $P_s$ over future outputs.  An $M$-state
   263→approximation merges causal states into at most $M$ lumped machine states
   264→through a stationary lumpable quotient.
   265→
   266→The full-KL retention gap is
   267→\[
   268→\RetKL(M)
   269→=
   270→\min_{\substack{
   271→\phi:\Splus\to\mathcal K\ \text{lumpable}\\
   272→|\mathcal K|\le M
   273→}}
   274→\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}
   275→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}
   276→\pi_s
   277→D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_s\|\bar P_k),
   278→\]
   279→where
   280→\[
   281→\bar P_k
   282→=
   283→\frac{
   284→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s P_s
   285→}{
   286→\sum_{s:\phi(s)=k}\pi_s
   287→}.
   288→\]
   289→In synchronized machines, lumpable quotients of $\Splus$ correspond to
   290→finite-index right congruences on sufficiently long histories.  In the general
   291→unifilar model the causal-state update reads the realized output, the feasible
   292→quotients are the unifilar-lumpable ones, and the input-driven synchronized
   293→subclass recovers right congruences on input histories.
   294→
   295→When the predictive family is restricted to common-covariance Gaussians, one
   296→obtains a quadratic restricted gap
   297→\[
   298→\RetQuad(M).
   299→\]
   300→The full-KL gap and the quadratic restricted gap are distinct objects.
   301→
   302→\emph{Positioning.}  The retention chapter extends the causal-state program of
   303→computational mechanics \cite{shalizi2001} from output processes to
   304→controlled, input-driven transductions: the objects approximated are lumpable
   305→quotients of a \emph{controlled} unifilar causal machine rather than
   306→partitions of a stationary process, and the feasibility notion of unifilar
   307→lumpability is correspondingly stricter than ordinary lumpability.  Unlike the
   308→information-bottleneck-for-causal-states line
   309→\cite{shalizicrutchfield2002,marzencrutchfield2014}, which characterizes the
   310→optimal causal statistic, and unlike the Kullback--Leibler aggregation line for
   311→regular Markov chains \cite{geiger2015,geiger2026}, which computes the optimal
   312→reduction, the manuscript tracks the full budget-$M$ gap curve: the zero
   313→threshold at the stable kernel refinement, NP-hardness below it, and spectral
   314→lower certificates from the predictive covariance, rather than only the
   315→optimum.
   316→
   317→\item \textbf{Grounding.}
   318→The target is a stochastic channel or residual kernel.  The spectral converse
   319→uses linear finite-rank realizations rather than deterministic Mealy
   320→machines.  The relevant object is a Hankel operator $H_\nu$ built from an
   321→impulse-response function.
   322→
   323→The unrestricted linear finite-rank Hankel relaxation gap is
   324→\[
   325→\Dunres(M)
   326→=
   327→\inf_{\operatorname{rank}B\le M}
   328→\norm{H_\nu-B}_{\mathrm{op}},
   329→\]
   330→and satisfies
   331→\[
   332→\Dunres(M)
   333→=
   334→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\nu)
   335→\]
   336→by Eckart--Young--Mirsky \cite{eckart1936,mirsky1960}.
   337→
   338→The Hankel-structured finite-rank realization gap is
   339→\[
   340→\DHankstr(M)
   341→=
   342→\inf_{\substack{
   343→\operatorname{rank}B\le M\\
   344→B\ \text{Hankel}
   345→}}
   346→\norm{H_\nu-B}_{\mathrm{op}},
   347→\]
   348→and satisfies
   349→\[
   350→\DHankstr(M)
   351→\ge
   352→\sigma_{M+1}(H_\nu).
   353→\]
   354→Equality in the Hankel-structured problem requires additional structure
   355→(Theorems~\ref{thm:aak-equality} and~\ref{thm:aak-multiletter}).  The
   356→resource $M$ here is a finite-rank budget on a bounded operator, not the
   357→index of a right congruence; Section~\ref{subsec:grounding-instantiation} and
   358→the master scope of Section~\ref{subsec:meta-scope} record this distinction
   359→explicitly, and Definition~\ref{def:symbolic-grounding-gap} below supplies a
   360→quotient-typed \emph{symbolic} grounding gap, over deterministic Mealy
   361→machines, whose resource is again a right-congruence index and which is
   362→therefore the object genuinely comparable to commitment and retention.  A
   363→closed-form finite-budget value for that symbolic gap remains open; certified
   364→bounds are given in
   365→Propositions~\ref{prop:grounding-finite-section},~\ref{prop:symbolic-finite-horizon}
   366→and Theorem~\ref{thm:observable-floor}.
   367→
   368→\emph{Positioning.}  The grounding chapter transports the
   369→Adamjan--Arov--Krein program for weighted automata
   370→\cite{balle2021,lacroce2024} from weighted languages to transductions.  The
   371→object approximated is the Hankel operator of a channel on the joint alphabet
   372→rather than that of a weighted language; the unrestricted and
   373→Hankel-restricted feasible sets are separated explicitly, the
   374→Eckart--Young--Mirsky value being available for the former and only the
   375→singular-value lower bound for the latter; and the published one-letter
   376→results extend to the multiletter setting only conditionally
   377→(Theorem~\ref{thm:aak-multiletter}, Open Problem~\ref{open:hankel-multiletter}),
   378→with grounded finite-section certificates and the zero-threshold
   379→characterization $\rank(H)\le M$ standing in for the exact minimization values
   380→of the one-letter theory.
   381→\end{enumerate}
   382→
   383→The three regimes share a \textbf{typed finite-memory budget} rather than a
   384→single literal resource.  For commitment and retention (and for the symbolic
   385→grounding gap of Definition~\ref{def:symbolic-grounding-gap}), the budget $M$
   386→is the index of a finite right congruence on histories.  For the
   387→\emph{unrestricted linear} grounding relaxation, the budget $M$ is instead the
   388→rank of a finite-rank operator approximating a Hankel operator.  These two
   389→notions of budget are related --- both cap the ``resolution'' of the
   390→approximant --- but they are not asserted equivalent: a rank-$M$ operator is
   391→not in general reachable through an $M$-state quotient, and an $M$-state
   392→quotient does not in general act on the joint alphabet the way a rank-$M$
   393→Hankel truncation does.  Section~\ref{subsec:meta-scope} makes this typed
   394→picture precise as four pillars common to all three regimes.
   395→
   396→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   397→\subsection{The shared finite-state schema}
   398→\label{subsec:shared-schema-intro}
   399→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   400→
   401→The shared combinatorial resource is the index of a finite quotient of a
   402→regime-typed history system (Definition~\ref{def:history-system}, developed in
   403→Section~\ref{subsec:history-actions}).
   404→Commitment quotients live on input histories $\mathcal I^*$ with a total right
   405→action; unifilar retention quotients live on feasible joint input--output
   406→histories with a pruned action, in the support-relative sense of
   407→Definition~\ref{def:support-right-cong}; grounding quotients live on the joint
   408→alphabet $\Sigma=\mathcal I\times\mathcal O$.  For the commitment regime, and
   409→for the input-driven retention subclass, the classical form is the following;
   410→Remark~\ref{rem:regime-history-instances} tabulates the three instantiations.
   411→
   412→Let $\mathcal I^*$ denote the set of finite input histories.  A right
   413→congruence $\sim$ on $\mathcal I^*$, in the standard sense of automata theory
   414→\cite{sakarovitch2009}, is an equivalence relation satisfying
   415→\[
   416→u\sim v
   417→\implies
   418→ux\sim vx
   419→\qquad
   420→\forall x\in\mathcal I.
   421→\]
   422→Equivalently, the quotient $\mathcal I^*/\!\sim$ carries a well-defined
   423→deterministic transition
   424→\[
   425→[u]\cdot x=[ux].
   426→\]
   427→The index of $\sim$ is the number of equivalence classes.  This index is the
   428→finite-state budget.
   429→
   430→A target sequential object may be represented as a trajectory
   431→\[
   432→\delta:\mathcal I^*\to\operatorname{ob}(\mathbf R),
   433→\]
   434→where $\mathbf R$ is a regime-specific residual category.  An $M$-state
   435→approximation factors $\delta$ through a finite quotient:
   436→\[
   437→\mathcal I^*
   438→\longrightarrow
   439→\mathcal I^*/\!\sim
   440→\longrightarrow
   441→\mathbf R,
   442→\qquad
   443→\operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M.
   444→\]
   445→The associated variational gap is
   446→\[
   447→\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)
   448→=
   449→\inf_{\substack{\sim,\Psi\\ \operatorname{index}(\sim)\le M}}
   450→\mathcal L(\delta,\Psi\circ Q).
   451→\]
   452→
   453→\begin{remark}[Scope of the Quotient Display]
   454→\label{rem:quotient-display-scope}
   455→The display above is the quotient-typed instance of the schema, and it is the
   456→literal resource for commitment, for retention, and for the symbolic
   457→grounding gap of Definition~\ref{def:symbolic-grounding-gap}.  The
   458→\emph{unrestricted linear} grounding relaxation of
   459→Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-grounding} instead instantiates the schema with a
   460→finite-rank feasible set,
   461→\[
   462→\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)
   463→=
   464→\inf_{\operatorname{rank}(B)\le M}
   465→\mathcal L(\delta,B),
   466→\]
   467→a genuinely different budget type.  Section~\ref{subsec:meta-scope} records
   468→both instances as coordinates of one typed variational schema without
   469→asserting that the two budgets coincide.
   470→\end{remark}
   471→
   472→Each regime attaches its own per-state semantic object:
   473→\begin{itemize}[nosep]
   474→\item \textbf{commitment:} a residual function;
   475→\item \textbf{retention:} a predictive distribution or exponential-family
   476→parameter;
   477→\item \textbf{grounding:} an operator, kernel, or linear realization
   478→coordinate.
   479→\end{itemize}
   480→
   481→The shared theorems concern the quotient structure and the index of right
   482→congruences.  The analytical engines---Myhill--Nerode theory, information
   483→bottleneck, Hankel approximation, game-theoretic commitment, online learning,
   484→and operator algebra---are regime-specific.
   485→
   486→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   487→\subsection{Stationary aggregation and type-correct costs}
   488→\label{subsec:stationary-intro}
   489→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   490→
   491→The average-case theory uses stationary aggregation.  For each fixed length
   492→$n$, a stationary process assigns total mass one to the prefixes of length $n$:
   493→\[
   494→\sum_{|u|=n}\mu(u)=1.
   495→\]
   496→Consequently,
   497→\[
   498→\sum_{u\in\mathcal I^*}\mu(u)=\infty,
   499→\]
   500→so the prefix weights do not define a probability measure on the whole free
   501→monoid $\mathcal I^*$.  The theory uses stationary aggregation schemes.
   502→
   503→Two canonical average-case aggregations are admitted:
   504→\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
   505→\item a stationary Ces\`aro average;
   506→\item a discounted prefix measure.
   507→\end{enumerate}
   508→
   509→The worst-case aggregation remains the supremum over all finite histories:
   510→\[
   511→\mathcal L_\infty(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   512→=
   513→\sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
   514→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\widehat\delta(u)).
   515→\]
   516→
   517→The cost profunctor $\mathcal E$ is regime-specific.  Commitment uses a
   518→Boolean or $0/1$ equality cost.  Retention uses KL divergence or a quadratic
   519→Fisher surrogate.  Grounding uses operator-norm distance or a related linear
   520→relaxation cost.  The structural Boolean cost $\{0,\infty\}$ is distinguished
   521→from the normalized operational cost $\{0,1\}$.
   522→
   523→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   524→\subsection{Analytical boundaries}
   525→\label{subsec:boundaries-intro}
   526→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   527→
   528→The framework separates shared syntax from regime-specific analysis.
   529→
   530→\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.0em]
   531→\item \textbf{Shared syntax versus regime-specific semantics.}
   532→The schema provides histories, right congruences, finite quotients, and
   533→variational gaps.  It does not derive the Myhill--Nerode theorem, the
   534→Eckart--Young--Mirsky theorem, or the information-bottleneck identity from
   535→categorical axioms alone.
   536→
   537→\item \textbf{Exact theorems versus local theorems.}
   538→The commitment zero-error threshold, the full-KL finite-state
   539→information-bottleneck identity, and the unrestricted linear Hankel spectral
   540→converse are exact within their domains.  The full-KL spectral lower bound is
   541→local and requires explicit regularity assumptions.  The Hankel-structured
   542→finite-rank realization gap satisfies the unrestricted singular-value lower
   543→bound, but equality requires additional structure.
   544→
   545→\item \textbf{Converses versus achievability.}
   546→Spectral converses are lower bounds on approximation gaps.  They become
   547→online regret upper rates only when paired with explicit approximation
   548→achievability bounds.
   549→
   550→\item \textbf{Linear relaxations versus discrete problems.}
   551→The linear Hankel gap and the linear Price-of-Safety surrogate are exact
   552→linear objects.  They do not automatically solve the corresponding discrete
   553→right-congruence optimization problems.
   554→
   555→\item \textbf{Heuristics versus theorems.}
   556→The spectral-tail picture is a heuristic.  The tail shape depends on
   557→aggregation, operator type, affine versus linear approximation, rank versus
   558→state count, compactness, domination, and quadratic-regime assumptions.
   559→
   560→\item \textbf{No unconditional cross-regime ordering.}
   561→The framework does not assert a universal inequality among grounding,
   562→retention, and commitment gaps.  The Independence Theorem shows that the
   563→gaps can be varied independently.
   564→\end{itemize}

   567→\subsection{Components of the framework}
   568→\label{subsec:integrated-components-intro}
   569→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   570→
   571→The framework comprises the following components.
   572→
   573→\paragraph{Schatten conditional converse template.}
   574→A task theory with a rank-controlled response operator $A_\delta$ and a
   575→Schatten-$p$ domination inequality satisfies a unified converse:
   576→\[
   577→\Delta_{\mathbb T}(M)
   578→\ge
   579→\frac1{c_p}
   580→\Phi^{(p)}_{r_{\mathbb T}(M)}(A_\delta),
   581→\]
   582→where $r_{\mathbb T}(M)$ is the effective rank budget and
   583→$\Phi^{(p)}_r$ is the Schatten-$p$ tail.
   584→
   585→Grounding is the Schatten-$\infty$ instance for the unrestricted linear
   586→finite-rank relaxation; retention is the Schatten-$1$ instance with effective
   587→rank $M-1$; commitment is the Boolean-rank vertex.  Contextwise $\ell^p$
   588→aggregation does not by itself imply Schatten-$p$ domination.
   589→
   590→\paragraph{Adaptive two-axis oracle inequality.}
   591→Within each type-correct regime, offline approximation and online estimation
   592→compose into a bias--variance decomposition:
   593→\[
   594→\Reg_T(\mathcal A_M)
   595→\le
   596→\Aapp^{\mathsf r}(M)
   597→+
   598→\Est_M^{\mathsf r}(T).
   599→\]
   600→Adaptive aggregation over the state budget $M$ yields an oracle inequality
   601→without knowledge of the optimal budget.  For bounded agnostic losses, the
   602→adaptive penalty is
   603→\[
   604→\PsiAgg_M(T)
   605→=
   606→O\!\left(
   607→\sqrt{T\ln(eM)}
   608→+
   609→\ln(eM)
   610→\right).
   611→\]
   612→Realizable $0/1$ and mixable log-loss model selection have logarithmic
   613→overhead.  Spectral tails enter online rates only under explicit spectral-rate
   614→assumptions.
   615→
   616→Estimation rates may be supplied by a Littlestone bound, a multiclass
   617→Littlestone bound, a mixability bound, or a linear-regret bound.  The oracle
   618→inequality does not assume a single estimation rate across regimes.
   619→
   620→\paragraph{Linear Price-of-Safety surrogate.}
   621→For a quadratic/Fisher retention embedding with state-indexed centered Gram
   622→matrix
   623→\[
   624→G_{st}
   625→=
   626→\sqrt{\pi_s\pi_t}\,
   627→\langle y_s-\bar y,\;y_t-\bar y\rangle,
   628→\]
   629→and a safety partition inducing a pinching conditional expectation
   630→$\EA$, the linear block-local Price-of-Safety surrogate is
   631→\[
   632→\PoSlin(M)
   633→=
   634→\frac12\KyF{G}{M-1}
   635→-
   636→\frac12\KyF{\EA G}{M-1}.
   637→\]
   638→Pinching majorization gives nonnegativity:
   639→\[
   640→\PoSlin(M)\ge0.
   641→\]
   642→The coupling bound
   643→\[
   644→\PoSlin(M)
   645→\le
   646→\frac12\KyF{G-\EA G}{M-1}
   647→\]
   648→is an upper bound, not an equality.  This surrogate is exact for the linear
   649→block-local problem but is not the discrete right-congruence Price of Safety
   650→without relaxation-error control.
   651→
   652→\paragraph{Exponent vertex correspondence.}
   653→The manuscript separates three exponent-like layers:
   654→\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
   655→\item R\'enyi order $\alpha$;
   656→\item Schatten exponent $p$;
   657→\item aggregation or vertex label.
   658→\end{enumerate}
   659→
   660→Retention is the $\alpha=1$ KL vertex.  Commitment is recovered from the
   661→$\alpha=0$ support vertex after symmetrization and Boolean valuation.
   662→Grounding is organized by the $\alpha=\infty$ supremum vertex, but the exact
   663→unrestricted linear finite-rank grounding cost is operator-norm distance, not
   664→max-divergence.
   665→
   666→The equality of vertex labels
   667→\[
   668→\alpha=p\in\{0,1,\infty\}
   669→\]
   670→is a formal consistency, not a derivation of the Mirsky converses from
   671→R\'enyi identities.
   672→
   673→\paragraph{Temporal protocols.}
   674→On the deterministic Mealy classes $\mathcal H_M$, the reset-word,
   675→persistent-stream, and active residual-identification protocols each have
   676→mistake complexity $\Theta(M\log M)$; the persistent-stream and active
   677→lower bounds are explicit forcing constructions rather than Littlestone
   678→transfers, and agnostic regret is $\Theta(\sqrt{TM\log M})$.

   710→\section{The Schema of Finite-State Approximation}
   711→\label{sec:schema}
   712→%======================================================================
   713→
   714→This section develops the shared finite-state schema.  The schema supplies a
   715→common syntax for all regimes: histories, enriched cost categories, finite
   716→right congruences, quotient machines, stationary aggregation, and profinite
   717→completion.  The analytical meaning of the residual objects is supplied later
   718→by each regime.

   720→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   721→\subsection{The Enriching Poset}
   722→\label{subsec:enriching-poset}
   723→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   724→
   725→All costs take values in the monoidal poset
   726→\[
   727→\mathbf V=([0,\infty],\ge,+,0).
   728→\]
   729→The unit is $0$, and the order is reversed from the usual real order: a
   730→morphism $v\to v'$ exists iff $v\ge v'$ in the usual order.  Thus $0$ is
   731→terminal and $\infty$ is initial.  A morphism $v\to v'$ records that the cost
   732→has decreased from $v$ to $v'$.
   733→
   734→The Boolean sub-poset is
   735→\[
   736→\mathbf V_{\mathbb B}=\{0,\infty\}\subset[0,\infty].
   737→\]
   738→It corresponds to exact equality versus definite mismatch.  A normalized
   739→Boolean mismatch cost with values $\{0,1\}$ is treated separately as an
   740→operational corollary.  The structural zero-error theory uses
   741→$\{0,\infty\}$; the normalized decision-theoretic version uses $\{0,1\}$.
   742→
   743→The reversed-order convention is the standard Lawvere metric convention
   744→\cite{lawvere1973}.
   745→
   746→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   747→\subsection{$\mathbf V$-Categories and $\mathbf V$-Profunctors}
   748→\label{subsec:V-cats-profunctors}
   749→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   750→
   751→A \textbf{$\mathbf V$-category} $\mathbf R$ consists of a set of objects
   752→$\operatorname{ob}(\mathbf R)$ and hom-objects
   753→\[
   754→\mathbf R(X,Y)\in\mathbf V
   755→\]
   756→satisfying:
   757→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
   758→\item \textbf{Identity.}
   759→\[
   760→0\ge \mathbf R(X,X),
   761→\]
   762→hence $\mathbf R(X,X)=0$.
   763→
   764→\item \textbf{Composition.}
   765→\[
   766→\mathbf R(X,Y)+\mathbf R(Y,Z)\ge \mathbf R(X,Z).
   767→\]
   768→\end{enumerate}
   769→
   770→A \textbf{$\mathbf V$-profunctor}
   771→\[
   772→\mathcal E:\mathbf R^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbf R\to\mathbf V
   773→\]
   774→assigns costs $\mathcal E(X,Y)$ with left and right actions satisfying
   775→\[
   776→\mathbf R(X',X)+\mathcal E(X,Y)\ge \mathcal E(X',Y),
   777→\]
   778→and
   779→\[
   780→\mathcal E(X,Y)+\mathbf R(Y,Y')\ge \mathcal E(X,Y').
   781→\]
   782→
   783→The profunctor $\mathcal E$ is the cost bimodule.  It measures the mismatch
   784→between residual objects $X$ and $Y$, while the left and right actions express
   785→compatibility with the internal geometry of $\mathbf R$.
   786→
   787→\begin{remark}[Discrete versus enriched residuals]
   788→\label{rem:discrete-vs-enriched}
   789→For retention and commitment, $\mathbf R$ is usually a discrete
   790→$\mathbf V$-category:
   791→\[
   792→\mathbf R(X,Y)=
   793→\begin{cases}
   794→0,&X=Y,\\
   795→\infty,&X\neq Y.
   796→\end{cases}
   797→\]
   798→Then the action inequalities impose no triangle inequality on $\mathcal E$.
   799→KL divergence is not a metric, and Boolean mismatch is not a metric.
   800→
   801→For grounding, $\mathbf R$ may be a metric $\mathbf V$-category of operators,
   802→with
   803→\[
   804→\mathbf R(X,Y)=
   805→\norm{X-Y}_{\mathrm{op}}.
   806→\]
   807→If $\mathcal E$ is the identity profunctor,
   808→\[
   809→\mathcal E(X,Y)=\mathbf R(X,Y),
   810→\]
   811→then the action inequalities reduce to the triangle inequality for the
   812→operator norm.
   813→\end{remark}

   816→\subsection{The History $\mathbf V$-Category}
   817→\label{subsec:history-category}
   818→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   819→
   820→Let $\mathcal I$ be a finite input alphabet.  The \textbf{history
   821→$\mathbf V$-category} $\mathbf H$ is the discrete $\mathbf V$-category on the
   822→free monoid $\mathcal I^*$:
   823→\begin{itemize}[nosep]
   824→\item objects are finite words $u\in\mathcal I^*$;
   825→\item hom-objects are
   826→\[
   827→\mathbf H(u,v)=
   828→\begin{cases}
   829→0,&u=v,\\
   830→\infty,&u\neq v.
   831→\end{cases}
   832→\]
   833→\end{itemize}
   834→
   835→Because $\mathbf H$ is discrete, any object-level map
   836→\[
   837→\delta:\mathcal I^*\to\operatorname{ob}(\mathbf R)
   838→\]
   839→is automatically a $\mathbf V$-functor.  No nontrivial morphism inequalities
   840→need to be checked.
   841→
   842→The prefix structure of histories is not encoded in the hom-objects of
   843→$\mathbf H$.  It is encoded externally in the right-congruence condition,
   844→which ensures that deterministic finite-state approximations evolve
   845→consistently under input extensions.

   847→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   848→\subsection{Task Theory with Stationary Aggregation}
   849→\label{subsec:task-theory}
   850→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   851→
   852→\begin{definition}[Task Theory]
   853→\label{def:task-theory}
   854→A \textbf{task theory} is a tuple
   855→\[
   856→\mathbb T=(\mathbf H,\mathbf R,\mathcal E,\delta,\mathrm{Agg},\mathcal L)
   857→\]
   858→where:
   859→\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\arabic*)]
   860→\item $\mathbf H$ is the history $\mathbf V$-category.
   861→\item $\mathbf R$ is a $\mathbf V$-category of residual objects.
   862→\item $\mathcal E:\mathbf R^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbf R\to\mathbf V$ is a
   863→cost profunctor which is \textbf{reflexive}: $\mathcal E(X,X)=0$ for every
   864→$X\in\mathbf R$.  Reflexivity is not automatic for a $\mathbf V$-profunctor.
   865→In the Lawvere quantale $([0,\infty],\ge,+)$ the constant profunctor
   866→$\mathcal E\equiv1$ on a one-object $\mathbf R$ satisfies every profunctor
   867→axiom, and for it the trajectory cannot be realized at any budget even though
   868→its Myhill--Nerode index is $1$.  Every cost used in this manuscript --- KL divergence,
   869→operator norm, and the $0/1$ commitment loss --- is reflexive, so the axiom
   870→constrains nothing in the instances but is required for the generic statements
   871→of Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:monotone}.
   872→\item $\delta:\mathbf H\to\mathbf R$ is the true trajectory.
   873→\item $\mathrm{Agg}$ is an aggregation scheme, one of:
   874→\begin{itemize}[nosep]
   875→\item a stationary Ces\`aro average;
   876→\item a discounted prefix measure; or
   877→\item the worst-case supremum
   878→$\mathcal L_\infty(\delta,\widehat\delta)=\sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
   879→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\widehat\delta(u))$.
   880→\end{itemize}
   881→The first two are \emph{average-case} schemes and require the stationary or
   882→discounted datum; the third requires none.  A task theory carrying the
   883→worst-case scheme is called \emph{uniform}.  Results are stated against a
   884→declared scheme, since the Boolean dichotomy in particular takes different
   885→forms under worst-case and average-case aggregation
   886→(Meta-Theorem~\ref{meta:boolean}).
   887→\item $\mathcal L$ is the resulting cost functional.
   888→\end{enumerate}
   889→\end{definition}
   890→
   891→\begin{definition}[Stationary Ces\`aro aggregation]
   892→\label{def:cesaro-agg}
   893→Let $U_t$ denote the stationary past at time $t$.  For a cost process
   894→\[
   895→c_t=\mathcal E(\delta(U_t),\widehat\delta(U_t)),
   896→\]
   897→define
   898→\[
   899→\overline{\mathcal L}_1(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   900→=
   901→\lim_{T\to\infty}
   902→\frac1T
   903→\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
   904→\mathbb E[c_t],
   905→\]
   906→whenever the limit exists.
   907→\end{definition}
   908→
   909→For finite-state stationary ergodic processes and finite-state
   910→approximations, the limit exists under standard ergodicity assumptions.  In
   911→non-ergodic settings, one may replace the limit by a limsup or by ergodic
   912→decomposition.
   913→
   914→\begin{definition}[Discounted prefix aggregation]
   915→\label{def:discounted-agg}
   916→For $0<\gamma<1$, define a probability measure on $\mathcal I^*$ by
   917→\[
   918→\mu_\gamma(u)
   919→=
   920→(1-\gamma)\gamma^{|u|}
   921→\mathbb P(X_{1:|u|}=u).
   922→\]
   923→Then
   924→\[
   925→\mathcal L_1^\gamma(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   926→=
   927→\mathbb E_{u\sim\mu_\gamma}
   928→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\widehat\delta(u)).
   929→\]
   930→\end{definition}
   931→
   932→The discounted prefix measure is a probability measure because
   933→\[
   934→\sum_{u\in\mathcal I^*}\mu_\gamma(u)
   935→=
   936→(1-\gamma)
   937→\sum_{n=0}^\infty
   938→\gamma^n
   939→\sum_{|u|=n}
   940→\mathbb P(X_{1:n}=u)
   941→=
   942→(1-\gamma)
   943→\sum_{n=0}^\infty
   944→\gamma^n
   945→=
   946→1.
   947→\]
   948→
   949→The worst-case aggregation remains
   950→\[
   951→\mathcal L_\infty(\delta,\widehat\delta)
   952→=
   953→\sup_{u\in\mathcal I^*}
   954→\mathcal E(\delta(u),\widehat\delta(u)).
   955→\]
   956→It is not an average-case aggregation and should not be conflated with the
   957→stationary Ces\`aro or discounted prefix schemes.
   958→
   959→\begin{remark}[Finite Prefixes versus Stationary States]
   960→\label{rem:prefixes-versus-states}
   961→Finite prefixes do not define a probability measure on all of
   962→$\mathcal I^*$.  For stationary retention, the canonical object is the
   963→stationary state process $(S_t)$ and its stationary support $\Splus$.  Finite
   964→histories enter directly in synchronized machines, where $S_t$ is a function
   965→of a finite suffix of the input past.
   966→\end{remark}

   969→\subsection{Regime-Typed History Actions}
   970→\label{subsec:history-actions}
   971→%----------------------------------------------------------------------
   972→
   973→The finite-state resource shared by the three regimes is the index of a finite
   974→quotient of a history system.  Which history system is regime-dependent: the
   975→regimes share the \emph{form} of a finite-index right-action quotient, not a
   976→single literal free monoid.  Forcing every regime onto $\mathcal I^*$ would
   977→misdescribe predictive machines whose state update reads the realized output.
   978→
   979→\begin{definition}[History System]
   980→\label{def:history-system}
   981→A \textbf{history system} is a tuple
   982→\[
   983→\mathsf H=(H,h_\varnothing,\mathcal A,\cdot),
   984→\]
   985→where $H$ is a set of finite histories with root $h_\varnothing$, $\mathcal A$
   986→is an event alphabet, and $\cdot$ is a partial right action
   987→\[
   988→H\times\mathcal A\rightharpoonup H,
   989→\qquad
   990→(h,a)\mapsto h\cdot a,
   991→\]
   992→defined exactly when $a$ is \emph{feasible} from $h$; write
   993→$\mathrm{Feas}(h)\subseteq\mathcal A$ for the set of feasible events.  The
   994→action is \emph{pruned}: whenever $h\cdot a$ is defined it lies in $H$.  If
   995→$\mathrm{Feas}(h)=\mathcal A$ for every $h$, the action is \emph{total}.
   996→\end{definition}
   997→
   998→\begin{definition}[Support-Relative Right Congruence]
   999→\label{def:support-right-cong}
  1000→Let $\mathsf H=(H,h_\varnothing,\mathcal A,\cdot)$ be a history system.  A
  1001→\textbf{support-relative right congruence} on $\mathsf H$ is an equivalence
  1002→relation $\sim$ on $H$ such that $u\sim v$ implies
  1003→\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),nosep]
  1004→\item $\mathrm{Feas}(u)=\mathrm{Feas}(v)$; and
  1005→\item $u\cdot a\sim v\cdot a$ for every $a\in\mathrm{Feas}(u)$.
  1006→\end{enumerate}
  1007→Its \textbf{index} is the number of equivalence classes.  The quotient
  1008→$H/\!\sim$ carries a well-defined partial transition $[u]\cdot a=[u\cdot a]$,
  1009→with $\mathrm{Feas}([u])=\mathrm{Feas}(u)$; clause~(i) is exactly what makes
  1010→the feasible-event set of a class well defined.  For a total action clause~(i)
  1011→is vacuous and the definition reduces to Definition~\ref{def:right-cong}.
  1012→\end{definition}
  1013→
  1014→\begin{remark}[Clause~(i) Is Not Redundant]
  1015→\label{rem:support-clause-i-needed}
  1016→Clause~(ii) alone, read as a condition on commonly feasible events, does not
  1017→give a quotient history system.  Take $\mathcal A=\{a,b\}$ and two histories
  1018→$u,v$ with $\mathrm{Feas}(u)=\{a\}$ and $\mathrm{Feas}(v)=\{b\}$.  The
  1019→relation identifying $u$ with $v$ satisfies clause~(ii) vacuously, there being
  1020→no commonly feasible event, yet the class $[u]$ has no well-defined feasible
  1021→set and the quotient carries no transition structure.  This is the mechanism
  1022→behind Remark~\ref{rem:unifilar-support-not-automatic} below.
  1023→\end{remark}
