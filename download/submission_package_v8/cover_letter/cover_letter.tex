The Editors\\
\emph{Information and Computation}

\bigskip

\textbf{Re: Submission of a regular research article --- ``The
Rate--Distortion Theory of Bounded Sequential Transduction: A Comparative
Syntax for Finite-State Approximation''}

\medskip

Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript referenced above for consideration as a
regular research article in \emph{Information and Computation}. The
manuscript develops a rate--distortion theory of bounded sequential
transduction in which the rate is a state budget $M$ and the distortion is a
regime-specific task loss, and it spans precisely the journal's stated scope:
theoretical computer science together with computational applications of
information theory. The work is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration by any other journal. The submission comprises
the manuscript (238 pages, 18 sections, 390 theorem-like environments of
which 141 carry proofs, with a 39-entry bibliography and a back-matter
notation index) and a supplementary package, both described below.

\textbf{What the paper establishes.} The manuscript gives one comparative
syntax for finite-state approximation across three regimes that until now
have been studied with disjoint instruments. Commitment targets deterministic
specifications through Mealy machines and Myhill--Nerode residuals; retention
targets stochastic controlled processes through stationary controlled
unifilar causal machines and their lumpable quotients; grounding targets
stochastic channels through linear finite-rank Hankel realizations, whose
resource is operator rank rather than quotient index. All three share one
syntactic core --- histories, finite-index right congruences, quotient
machines, stationary aggregation, and variational gaps --- while their
semantic objects differ, and every approximation claim carries an explicit
type signature fixing regime, feasible set, aggregation, support, protocol,
and analytical bridge, so that statements from different regimes cannot be
silently conflated.

\textbf{The contributions we would foreground.} First, the comparative
syntax itself, together with its negative results: an independence theorem
shows that the three regimes' approximation obstructions vary
independently, so no cross-regime ordering of gaps holds. Second, the type
discipline: seven-coordinate signatures that make regime boundaries
mechanically checkable, which is what allows the comparative statements to
be stated at all. Third, the determination-index ladder
($\kappa_{\mathrm{pair}}$, $\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}$,
$\kappa_{\mathrm{det}}$), which organizes commitment-side approximability;
exact-phrase searches over arXiv and the open web (2026-09-02) confirm the
terms enter the literature unclaimed. Fourth, the protocol stratification:
mistake complexity $\Theta(M\log M)$ in the reset-word, persistent-stream,
and active residual-identification protocols, with agnostic regret
$\Theta(\sqrt{TM\log M})$. On the retention side the paper also proves an
exact information-bottleneck decomposition, NP-completeness in the Gaussian
quadratic restricted regime, and a sharp spectral converse; on the grounding
side it identifies the unrestricted gap with $\sigma_{M+1}$ by
Eckart--Young--Mirsky and a superpolynomial determinism gap.

\textbf{Positioning.} I would state plainly that the per-regime instruments
are, for the most part, transports of known tools --- information-bottleneck
decompositions, spectral converses, singular-value approximation,
minimax lower-bound techniques --- into the unified accounting. The
contribution is the comparative layer: the shared schema, the independence
and non-orderability results, the type discipline, and the exact constants
that become meaningful only when the regimes are placed side by side. The
manuscript's introduction positions the work against the automata
minimization, computational-mechanics, and Hankel/AAK operator-theoretic
literatures, and its coined terms carry disambiguation footnotes where
neighboring literatures use similar phrases.

\textbf{Length and referee navigation.} The manuscript is long, and it was
engineered for that: a table of contents, a notation index of four
cross-referenced tables resolving roughly fifty bespoke symbols to their
defining sites, consolidated cross-references, and theorem numbering within
sections. The three regimes are individually self-contained chapters, so a
referee can navigate by the section map on page one. A preprint of this
version will be posted to arXiv concurrent with the submission, to timestamp
the coined vocabulary and the results while review proceeds.

\textbf{The multiletter-AAK status, stated honestly.} The most exposed
single claim concerns the Hankel-structured grounding gap for multilet
alphabets. The manuscript proves a conditional-form theorem: the transport
argument is proved, and its conclusion holds under a structure-preserving
embedding hypothesis that is recorded as an explicit open problem, not as an
established fact. This material has been internally proof-checked to the
line level, its supporting computational claims have been independently
reproduced, and it has no external corroboration~--- which is the documented
reason it received a dedicated check. The abstract and the theorem's
scope remarks state the conditional status explicitly, so no downstream
claim overstates it.

\textbf{Verification depth.} Two dedicated proof-check reports accompany
this submission as optional editor material; between them they record
line-level checks of all 141 proof-bearing results, including the
multiletter-AAK material. The supplementary package converts every quoted
computational observation into a runnable check: a 41-check numerical
verification suite, the exhaustive enumeration programs with their exact
outputs and extremal machine tables, and the Lean 4 development of the
machine-checked fragment --- seventeen statements across seven modules
against Mathlib, shipped with a build script and an axiom-audit gate that
recompiles the development and verifies every tracked declaration against
Lean's standard three axioms. The manuscript's availability statement
describes all of this, and the supplementary README documents the three
places where reproduction is partial or not re-run, with reasons.

\textbf{Declarations.} The author received no funding for this work and
declares no competing interests. Generative-AI assistance in drafting and
manuscript preparation is declared in the manuscript's back matter
(``Acknowledgments and Declaration of Generative AI''), where the author's
responsibility for the final content is stated. No empirical dataset was
used.

Thank you for considering the manuscript. I would be glad to provide any
further material that would help the review.
